# NIGHTSIDE  (pitch:flow)

**Hook:** You swing from satellite to satellite over a neon ring-city trying to outrun the sunrise: there is no health bar, your speed is your life, and every gear you shift adds another layer to the soundtrack.

**Fantasy:** You are a slinger, a courier who rides the mag-rails and satellite tethers of Nocturne, a ring-city in orbit that only lives on its night side. The sun-shield has failed and dawn is sweeping around the ring at the speed of its spin, turning the city to embers behind you. You cannot fight it or hide from it; you can only keep your line clean enough to stay faster than the light, and the score is how long you made the night last.

**Perspective:** Side-on 2D, orthographic, six parallax layers. The runner is pinned 30% from the left edge so 70% of the frame is look-ahead; the camera follows vertically with a soft dead zone and leads toward the predicted landing point. Zoom is tied to gear (1.00, 0.94, 0.87, 0.80, 0.72: 48 m to 67 m of visible width at 16:9, 40 px per metre at 1080p in gear 1), pulls back to 0.60 for big air, rolls up to 2.5 deg into swings and springs on impacts. The play space is four horizontal tiers: the hull at y=0 and rails at 4, 8 and 12 m, with beacons floating 5 to 11 m above the rail they serve. Everything is built from primitives: rails are polylines extruded to quads (3 px white core, cyan halo, bloom) with per-vertex displacement so they ripple like strings; beacons are ring line-loops with a crosshair and an additive glow sprite; the hull is a matte dark strip with violet seams; the runner is 14 primitives (hex head, torso quad, two-segment limbs, a glowing board line) blended between six poses, plus a 40-point ribbon scarf; lightfalls are vertical gradient quads with scrolling stripes and a hot core; wardens are filled inverted triangles with a lime cap; the skyline is dark quads with window dots; the planet is a triangle-fan disc with band quads and ellipse ring lines; the far arc of the ring is a polyline whose vertices brighten as day reaches them; the dawn is a gradient quad with a shader-noise edge, heat-haze distortion and ember particles.

**Core loop:** Second to second (one loop is about one bar at 128 BPM): read the next lime ring and the next cyan line -> UP to leave the rail -> HOLD to latch the beacon and swing for one beat -> RELEASE as the tether turns white -> pick your landing and press DOWN to spear into it -> SLAM, then tap UP inside the rebound window to be thrown back into the air toward the next beacon. Height becomes speed (slam), speed becomes height (rebound, sling), and every clean link drops pips into the rev meter. Fill it and you shift up a gear: faster, wider camera, longer scarf, one more layer of music. Touch the floor or anything hot and you drop a gear, the mix loses a layer, and the dawn behind you gets closer, your own shadow stretching out ahead of you to tell you so. Run to run: the only way to die is to be caught, so every death is a story about the exact link where you lost your speed, and restart takes one second. Goals stack by themselves: reach gear 3, clear sector 3, hold gear 5 ('escape velocity') for a whole sector, beat your distance. After the authored cold open, sectors are assembled from seeded hand-made patterns, so the route is fresh every run while the grammar never changes.

## Controls
- **UP = Space, W, Up arrow or left mouse (touch: right half of the screen). Tapped while on a rail or the hull.** → JUMP. Fixed arc: 4.5 m apex, 0.85 s airtime, 120 ms input buffer, 100 ms coyote time. Rails are one-way (pass up through them, land on top), so a jump is also 'up one tier'. Tapped within 150 ms after a SLAM, or out of a hull skid, it becomes a REBOUND with a 9 m apex (two tiers).  
  _Feedback:_ Same frame: the runner squashes for 2 frames then stretches, a ring of sparks puffs from the board, the rail dips and twangs like a plucked string (a visible wave runs along it), and a triangle chirp rises 330 to 660 Hz. Rebound: the rail visibly bows 0.6 m and whips back, a white afterimage column trails the runner, the chirp is an octave higher over a sub thump, and the camera lifts ahead of you.
- **UP held while airborne** → SLING latch. Tethers the highlighted beacon the instant it is in range (you may hold early, so jump-and-hold latches by itself). You swing a circular arc beneath it for about one beat.  
  _Feedback:_ A lime line cracks out to the beacon in 2 frames, jagged then snapping taut; the beacon ring flares and spins up; the camera rolls 2.5 deg into the swing and zooms out 6%; the scarf trail doubles in width and paints the arc. FM 'zap-twang' on latch, then a riser that climbs through the swing. The tether heats from lime to white as the sweet zone arrives, with a bright 'ting' and a flash on the runner the moment it opens. A dotted ghost arc shows where letting go now would land you (grey = floor, lime = rail or beacon, white = whipcrack). With no beacon in range the tether flicks out 3 m and recoils with a soft 'fwip', so the press is never dead.
- **UP released while swinging** → FLING. You leave on the tangent: forward speed = gear speed, vertical speed 12 to 21 m/s depending on how late you let go. Graded GOOD, SLING, WHIPCRACK or LATE by release angle.  
  _Feedback:_ The tether snaps and whips away, a shockwave ring distorts the screen from the release point, hitstop of 40 ms (SLING) or 70 ms (WHIPCRACK), chromatic aberration spikes to 6 px and decays in 200 ms, speed lines for 400 ms, pips slam into the rev meter, and a small callout appears at the runner. Sound: a noise whip-crack, a 120 to 40 Hz sub drop and a chord stab in key; WHIPCRACK adds a double sonic-boom thump and a rising sparkle arpeggio. LATE: the tether goes grey and limp, a detuned falling blip plays, and you visibly stall in the air.
- **DOWN = S or Down arrow (touch: left half of the screen). Pressed while airborne.** → DIVE. Vertical speed becomes -38 m/s on that frame, forward speed untouched. Ends in a SLAM (rail), a STOMP (top of a warden) or a floor hit.  
  _Feedback:_ The runner snaps to a spear pose with three white afterimages and an air-ring at the start point; a falling noise-whoosh sweeps 4 kHz to 500 Hz. SLAM: 50 ms hitstop, the camera kicks 10 px down on a spring, a radial shockwave ripples the screen out to 6 m, sparks fan both ways along the rail, the rail bows and rings a bass note from the current chord, and the kick drum ducks for 100 ms so the impact owns the mix. STOMP: the drone pops into triangle shards with a glassy shatter and a note that climbs the F minor pentatonic with each consecutive stomp.
- **DOWN pressed while grinding a rail or running on the hull** → DROP-THROUGH on a rail: the rail opens and you dive to the tier below (it SLAMs if that rail is still charged). On the hull: a 0.35 s SKID that loads a REBOUND for the next UP.  
  _Feedback:_ The rail segment under your feet blinks out for 150 ms with a downward 'zip' and you spear through it; the landing gets the full SLAM treatment. Skid: the runner drops low, throws a fan of grey sparks with a gritty scrape, and the board glows as the spring loads.
- **Any key on the title screen or the death screen** → Start or restart the run  
  _Feedback:_ Title letters shatter and are sucked off to the left, a sub 'thoom' lands, and you have control in 0.6 s on the next kick. After a death: a reverse whoosh as night wipes back across the white frame, and you are grinding again within about 1 s.

## Mechanics
### SLING (tether swing)
- Rules: While airborne, holding UP latches the nearest beacon that is ahead of you, at least 2 m above you and within 1.35 x the nominal rope length; the latch is instant, and you may hold early so it latches the moment the beacon comes into range. Nominal rope length is 0.34 x gear speed (5 m in gear 1, 11 m in gear 5); actual length is your distance at latch, clamped to 0.8 to 1.35 x nominal. The swing is kinematic, not simulated: you travel the circular arc under the beacon on a fixed angular profile (2.6 rad/s with the rope horizontal, 3.55 rad/s at the bottom), so a swing from the authored latch point (55 deg before the bottom) to the centre of the sweet zone takes exactly one beat (469 ms) in every gear and averages 1.03 x cruise speed over the ground: latching never costs speed. Release zones by angle past the bottom of the arc: before 5 deg = BAIL (flat, short, 0 pips); 5 to 50 = GOOD (about 230 ms, 1 pip, and the launch is aim-assisted by up to 12% so it always reaches the next rail or the next beacon's latch zone); 25 to 50 = SLING (about 130 ms, centred on the beat, 2 pips); 41 to 50 = WHIPCRACK (the last 48 ms, about 3 frames, 3 pips); past 50 = LATE (the tether greys out and you are lobbed at 24 m/s vertical with only 40% forward speed, recovering over 0.6 s: a visible stall that hands the dawn 4 to 6 m, and the chain breaks); at 70 deg the tether lets go by itself. On a good release forward speed equals gear speed and vertical speed rises linearly from 12 m/s at 5 deg to 21 m/s at 50 deg. While tethered you pass through rails, but hot things still burn. A dotted ghost arc shows live where letting go now would send you: grey = floor, lime = rail or beacon, white = whipcrack.
- Why fun: It is the oldest joy in movement games, a pendulum you let go of, stripped to one button and made rhythmic: latch on a beat, let go on the next. The swing has a physical wind-up (riser, heating tether, camera roll) and a release the whole screen answers. The greed is built into the arc: every swing asks 'how late do you dare', and because the worst outcome sits directly behind the best one, even the hundredth swing is a decision.
- Interplay: A sling needs height under a beacon, which a plain jump barely gives and a REBOUND or kicker gives generously, so slams set up slings. A sling ends high and fast, which is exactly what a SLAM wants (a drop of 8 m or more is a METEOR). Slings are the largest pip source, so they are how you shift gears, and gears are how you stay ahead of the dawn. A LATE fling is the purest 'mistake costs momentum' in the design: you do not die, you hang in the air while the light gains on you.
### SLAM and REBOUND (dive)
- Rules: DOWN while airborne sets vertical speed to -38 m/s on that frame; forward speed is untouched. SLAM: ending a dive on a charged rail after a drop of at least 3 m pays 1 pip; a drop of 8 m or more is a METEOR and pays 2. Every rail spawns charged (chevrons flow along it) and pays once per visit, so one rail cannot be farmed. REBOUND: UP within 150 ms after any slam launches you at 30 m/s (9 m apex, two tiers) instead of the normal 21.2 m/s, and pays +1 pip if the slam itself paid. STOMP: coming down on the top half of a warden drone destroys it, pays 2 pips and bounces you at 24 m/s; touching it anywhere else is a burn. DOWN while grinding is a drop-through: the rail opens and you dive to the tier below (a 4 m drop, so it slams if that rail is charged). DOWN on the hull is a 0.35 s skid, and UP out of a skid is a rebound: the fast way from the floor back to tier 2. Ordinary landings are always safe: rails magnetise your feet from 0.6 m away and forgive 0.5 m past either end.
- Why fun: It hands the player the half of the arc that jump-only runners leave to gravity. You decide exactly when a flight ends, and ending it is the heaviest hit in the game: hitstop, a shockwave through the screen, the rail ringing like a bass string. Slam-then-rebound is a trampoline rhythm (down, UP) that feels like pumping a swing, and stomping a row of drones on the beat turns a hazard field into a drum fill.
- Interplay: Dive is the precision tool that makes it safe to be greedy with slings: overshoot a rail and you can still spear it. It converts the height a sling gives you into pips, and the rebound converts the impact back into the height the next sling needs: a closed loop of height -> speed -> height. It is also the only way to change tiers downward, which is how you reach the shadow under a lightfall, and the only way to turn a warden from an obstacle into a springboard.
### GEARS versus THE DAWN (speed is health)
- Rules: Five gears with fixed cruise speeds of 15, 19, 23, 27 and 32 m/s (gear 5 is 'escape velocity'); speed eases to a new gear over 0.4 s. Pips needed to shift up: 3, 5, 7, 9. Pips never drain on their own. You lose one gear and all pips when you touch anything hot (followed by 0.7 s of invulnerability) and at the moment you touch the hull; staying on the hull costs another gear every 0.8 s. In gear 1 a burn is a 0.4 s stumble at 10 m/s instead. Nothing kills you except the dawn: it starts 14 m behind you and moves at a speed set by the sector: 13.5, 16, 19.5, 23, 25.5, 28, 30.5, 32.5 m/s, then +0.5 per sector. Your lead changes by (your speed - its speed) every second and is capped at 46 m; at 0 you are caught. Each sector ends at an eclipse gate worth +8 m of lead. CLUTCH: within 10 m of the dawn, pips count double. Gear also sets the score multiplier (x1 to x5), camera zoom, scarf length and colour, bloom and saturation, and how many layers of the soundtrack are playing.
- Why fun: One number does everything. Going fast is not just safer, it is louder, wider, brighter and worth more, so the player's selfish urge (make the music kick in) and the survival rule (stay ahead of the light) point the same way. Because a mistake costs a gear instead of the run, the game never interrupts you: failure is a long, legible slide you can feel coming and can still rescue, and the rescues are the stories players tell.
- Interplay: Gears are fed only by slings, slams, rebounds and stomps, so the health system literally is the movement system. The floor rule makes rails and beacons the fast lane without a single instant-death pit. The dawn converts every lost gear into a visible, audible threat within a second, which gives the flow game real stakes without ever stopping the flow.

## First 30 seconds
- 0-2s: TITLE TO RUN. The logo hangs over live attract-mode play. Any key: the letters shatter into shards that are sucked off to the left, a sub 'thoom' lands, and you are already grinding a long cyan rail with sparks pouring off the board. Kick drum only, 128 BPM. No fade, no countdown; control in 0.6 s.
- 2-6s: THE THREAT. A thin amber line appears on the left horizon and swells. High in the sky, on the far arc of the ring, a white line is visibly crawling toward you. Then the dawn front slides into the left edge of the frame: towers behind you whiten, crack and blow away as embers, heat haze warps the picture, a low roar fades in, and your own shadow suddenly stretches out ahead of you along the rail. You are faster than it for now and it slowly falls back off-screen. Nothing has been explained and the whole premise is understood. The rail is safe, so this is where players try the button and feel the jump.
- 6-9s: JUMP. The last 3 m of the rail strobe on the beat and a bouncing keycap pictogram [UP] floats over the gap. Jump: chirp, squash and stretch, the rail twangs. Land on the next rail: spark burst, grind hiss. Miss it and you drop to the hull, the sparks go grey, the scarf droops, and a low on-ramp rail is 10 m ahead. Nothing can kill you in this sector: the dawn is slower than gear 1.
- 9-13s: FIRST SLING. The rail curls up into a kicker that launches you with no input. A lime ring-beacon hangs in the gap pulsing on the kick, a dotted arc previews the swing, and a [HOLD] keycap fills like a radial timer. Hold: the tether cracks out, the camera rolls and widens, the riser climbs, the tether heats to white. Release: whip-crack, sub drop, shockwave ring, 40 ms hitstop. The bass line enters on the release.
- 13-16s: THROUGH THE SIGN. The fling carries you straight through a 30 m magenta holo-koi swimming around a tower. It glitches and bursts into harmless shards around you (cool colours never hurt). A second beacon hangs just beyond it, exactly on your arc: if you are still holding, it latches by itself. Second sling, bigger than the first, with the ringed planet filling the sky behind your silhouette.
- 16-20s: FIRST SLAM. You are falling toward a fat charged rail with chevrons streaming along it; a [DOWN] keycap pulses above it. Dive: spear pose, afterimages. SLAM: hitstop, the screen ripples, the rail bows and rings a low F, sparks fan out both ways. The rev pips fill: GEAR 2. Hats and the 16th-note bass come in, the camera widens, the scarf lengthens and turns blue.
- 20-25s: THE CHAIN. Kicker, beacon, then a single hot-orange warden drone with a lime cap sitting directly under your arc. Dive on it: it pops into triangles and throws you back up into a third beacon. Three clean links in a row: the callouts stack, the chain counter ticks, and a decent first-timer hits GEAR 3. Snare and pad arrive and every window in the skyline starts pulsing with the kick.
- 25-30s: THE REVEAL. You land on a long rail that sweeps downhill and scoops into a giant kicker: launched 22 m above the rooftops. The camera pulls back to 0.6x, the music sinks under a low-pass to a breath of pad and wind, and for a second and a half of hang time you see everything: the whole ring curving up into the sky, the planet below, the white terminator line eating the city behind you. A lone satellite beacon sits at the apex. Hold, whip, release: you spear 14 m down into the neon canyon and the METEOR SLAM slams the filter open, dropping every layer you have earned back in on your own input. The eclipse gate irises shut behind you, the dawn roars and speeds up. The tutorial is over and nobody told you anything.

The apex sling of the big-air reveal. ## Hazards and patterns
Model: four horizontal tiers (hull at y=0, rails at 4, 8 and 12 m); every object is a line segment, a point or a box in a tier slot. Patterns are authored in beats at 128 BPM and stretched to metres by gear speed when they spawn, so the same input rhythm solves a pattern in every gear and clean play lands on the beat. Generator rules: chunk entry and exit tiers must match; every lightfall leaves a reachable tier in shadow; the hull always offers an on-ramp within 2 beats; each chunk is padded so its first event lands on a beat at your current gear; chunks already on screen are never rebuilt. VOCABULARY. RAIL: cyan line, one-way, frictionless, spawns 'charged' and pays one slam. KICKER: an up-curled rail end that launches you automatically at 26 m/s vertical (mega-kicker 47 m/s). BEACON: lime ring, the tether point. GAP: no rail on your tier; sling it, change tier, or fall to the hull and lose a gear. LIGHTFALL: a 3 to 5 m wide column of sunlight leaking through the city, covering 1 to 3 tiers; touching it is a burn; static at first, later pulsing 2 beats on and 2 beats off with a one-beat outline pre-flash. WARDEN: an overheated security drone, a hot orange inverted triangle with a lime cap; its sides and underside burn, its top is a STOMP and a bounce; static at first, later bobbing 4 m on a 2-beat cycle. SCORCHED HULL (sector 5 onward): amber, hazard-striped stretches of floor where landing costs 2 gears instead of 1. HOLO-SIGN: magenta wireframe billboards and creatures in the play plane; harmless, they shatter when you fly through them for +25. ECLIPSE GATE: the sector-end ring whose shutters buy +8 m of lead. EXAMPLE PATTERNS (length in beats). 1) ON-RAMP (8): hull, then a T1 rail, then an overlapping T2 rail; two hops take you from the floor to the fast lane. 2) FIRST LIGHT (8): T2 rail into a kicker, one beacon, a charged T2 rail to slam: latch on 2.5, release on 3.5, slam on 5. 3) STAIRCASE (8): three short charged rails stepping down T3, T2, T1 with half-beat gaps and a lightfall filling the air above each lower step, so floating is punished and you must dive each step: SLAM, SLAM, SLAM, then REBOUND off the last one up to the T3 exit. 4) MONKEY BARS (10): three beacons two beats apart over bare (later scorched) hull, exit on T3: latch 1, release 2, latch 3, release 4, latch 5, release 6, slam 7. Three whipcracks is 9 pips. 5) LOOM (12): three stacked rails with lightfalls at beats 3, 6 and 9 blocking (T2+T3), (hull+T1) and (T1+T2): drop-slam, rebound up, then drop again or sling over the third from the beacon above it. Solvable three ways, each drawing a different line. 6) POGO LINE (8): a 4-beat gap with three wardens one beat apart at T2 height: stomp, stomp, stomp on the beat for 6 pips and a rising note ladder, or take the single high beacon over the top for 2 to 3 pips. 7) CATHEDRAL (16, set piece, once every two sectors): a 5-beat downhill rail into a mega-kicker, a satellite beacon at the apex, a long charged rail for the METEOR SLAM, then 4 beats of calm. No hazards: pure vista and reward. 8) SPLIT (12, sector 4 onward): a high line of short T3 rails with two bobbing wardens and a beacon pair (up to 11 pips) against one unbroken T1 rail with no pips and a pulsing lightfall at its end; both rejoin on T2. 9) ECLIPSE GATE (4): a calm T2 rail through the ring; breathe, reset your eyes, go.

## Readability
Four laws, enforced in the renderer rather than left to taste. 1) HUE: warm means hurt. The amber-orange-white band is reserved for sunlight and the things it has heated (the dawn, lightfalls, wardens, scorched hull). Nothing else in the world may be warm: the planet is cold blue, signage is magenta and violet, sparks are white or the gear colour. Cyan is only ever a surface you can ride; lime is only ever something you can grab or stomp. 2) SHAPE: lines carry you, rings hold you, triangles and solid columns burn you. The code survives greyscale, so it is colour-blind safe. 3) VALUE: the play plane sits on a 35% dark scrim; background layers are capped at 45% luminance, drawn with 1 px lines and no filled cores, and the far layers are rendered at half resolution so they read soft. Only play-plane objects and the dawn exceed the bloom threshold, and rail cores, hazard outlines and the runner are redrawn after bloom in a crisp pass so glow never eats an edge. Hazards always have a hard white-hot core and animated stripes; decoration never has either. 4) MOTION AND TIME: decoration never moves in the play plane and never scrolls faster than 0.6x; there are no foreground occluders over the right 65% of the screen; the camera guarantees at least 1.4 s of look-ahead in every gear; every hazard flashes for 2 frames and ticks in the right speaker as it enters the screen, and pulsing hazards show an outline one beat before they fire. Post effects obey the same budget: chromatic aberration and distortion are radial and fall to zero in the look-ahead corridor in front of the runner. The dawn itself is read peripherally, never by looking back: left-edge glow, the mix filtering down, and your own shadow stretching forward into exactly the part of the screen you are already watching.

## Risk / reward
Score = metres travelled x gear (x1 to x5), plus move points x gear x chain multiplier. Moves: GOOD fling 50, SLING 100, WHIPCRACK 300, SLAM 100, METEOR 250, REBOUND 50, STOMP 150 (+50 for each consecutive stomp), near miss 75 (passing within 0.8 m of anything hot), holo-sign shatter 25, sector clear 1000 x sector number. The chain counts consecutive pip-earning moves and multiplies move points by 1 + 0.1 per link, up to x3; it breaks on a floor touch, a burn or a LATE fling. Greed loops: 1) The whipcrack cliff: every swing pays more the later you release, and the best 48 ms sits directly against the worst outcome (LATE: a stall and a broken chain). 2) High line or low line: the upper tiers carry the beacons, wardens and meteor drops; the low rail is safe and pays nothing, and the dawn turns 'safe' into a slow death. 3) Stomp routes: a warden is 2 pips and a free bounce if you hit its cap, and a lost gear if you clip its side. 4) Fresh rails: a rail pays its slam once, so hopping between short charged rails beats coasting along one long one. 5) Near-miss threading: diving through the gap beside a lightfall instead of changing tier early. 6) CLUTCH: within 10 m of the dawn pips count double, so the most dangerous place in the game is also where a comeback is fastest; it cannot be farmed, because the only way to get there is to have lost your gears. The end screen shows distance, score, top gear, best chain and sector reached, each against your best.

## Escalation
Sectors are 16 bars (30 s) and end at an eclipse gate. The dawn's speed is a staircase, and each step makes the gear that saved you last sector insufficient: S1 13.5 m/s (slower than gear 1, so nobody dies in the cold open), S2 16 (gear 2 gains), S3 19.5 (gear 2 bleeds 0.5 m/s, gear 3 gains), S4 23 (gear 3 only holds), S5 25.5 (gear 4 needed), S6 28 (gear 4 bleeds), S7 30.5 (gear 5 needed), S8 32.5 (even gear 5 bleeds), then +0.5 per sector: the sun always wins. Lead is capped at 46 m and each gate returns 8 m, so nobody banks permanent safety and nobody dies from one mistake. Resulting run lengths: never leaving gear 1 is about 65 s; averaging gear 2 about 1:40; gear 3 about 2:20; gear 4 about 3:05; a perfect gear 5 run ends just under 5 minutes. Content ramps with it: S1 long rails and single beacons (the authored cold open); S2 static lightfalls and wardens; S3 beacon chains and tier weaves; S4 bobbing wardens, pulsing lightfalls and split routes; S5 scorched hull appears and rails get shorter; S6 onward rails thin out until the route is mostly beacons, wardens and short charged rails, with lightfalls pulsing on the beat. Feel ramps too: cruise speed 15 to 32 m/s, camera zoom 1.00 to 0.72 (look-ahead time falls from 2.2 s to 1.46 s), bloom and saturation +10% per gear, the music from kick and sub alone to the full arrangement, and each sector re-colours the skyline. Intensity breathes rather than only climbing: every sector places one hazard-free set piece (the CATHEDRAL or the gate) directly after its densest pattern.

## Audio direction
128 BPM, F minor. A section: Fm - Db - Ab - Eb, two bars each (the 8-bar loop is 15 s, so a 16-bar sector is exactly 30 s); B section on even sectors: Db - Eb - Fm - Cm. INSTRUMENTS, all synthesized: kick (sine 150 to 45 Hz in 120 ms plus a 4 ms click, four on the floor); sub pulse (sine, 8th notes); bass (saw plus a square an octave down through an enveloped low-pass at 180 to 900 Hz, a 16th-note octave line, ducked 60% for 120 ms on every kick); hats (8 kHz band-passed noise, closed on the off-beats, open on the 'and' of 4); snare-clap (noise through a 1.8 kHz band-pass over a 190 Hz triangle body, short gated reverb); pad (three saws detuned 9 cents, 1.4 kHz low-pass with a slow LFO, two modulated delays as chorus); arp (pulse wave, 16ths across two octaves of chord tones, dotted-8th delay at 351 ms, feedback 0.35); lead (saw with 5.5 Hz vibrato and 60 ms glide, a 4-bar hook in F minor pentatonic); reverb from a generated 2.2 s noise impulse. ADAPTIVE LAYERING: every layer always runs in sync and is mixed by gain. Gear 1: kick, sub and dawn rumble. Gear 2: + hats and the 16th bass. Gear 3: + snare-clap and pad. Gear 4: + arp and open hats. Gear 5: + lead, master filter fully open, deeper sidechain. Shifting up unmutes immediately on a stinger (reverse noise swell into an impact), not at the next bar. Shifting down tape-stops the top layer and dunks the master low-pass. Big air sweeps the master low-pass to 500 Hz and raises the reverb send, and the landing slam opens it again, so the drop is triggered by the player. Dawn proximity under 15 m adds a noise roar and a rising Shepard tone; under 6 m an alarm pulses F against Gb on 8ths. SOUND EFFECTS are pitched to the current chord so play harmonises with the track: jump (triangle chirp 330 to 660 Hz, 70 ms); grind (resonant band-passed noise at 2 to 4 kHz tracking speed, FM 'zing' on touchdown); latch (FM pluck 'zap-twang' with a noise click); swing riser (saw climbing an octave from the chord root, filter opening); sweet-zone 'ting' (triangle on the fifth); fling (noise whip-crack, 120 to 40 Hz sub drop, chord stab; whipcrack adds a double boom and an upward arpeggio); dive (falling whoosh); slam (90 to 35 Hz thump, noise crunch, inharmonic FM ring, the rail's bass note); stomp (comb-filtered glass shatter plus a pentatonic note that climbs per stomp); burn (wave-shaped square burst and sizzle); gear up (three-note rise and a clunk); death (noise swell, hard cut, one long sub note in reverb).

## Failure and restart
One fail state: the dawn reaches you (lead = 0). Everything else (burns, the floor, late flings) only takes speed, so a run ends as the visible sum of your mistakes and never as a surprise. The catch is a 0.6 s gesture: 350 ms at 30% time as the white front crosses the runner, the silhouette goes black, cracks and blows forward as ash, the frame blooms to white and the music collapses to one held sub note. The result is already on screen in huge type over the white: distance, sector, top gear and best chain, each with a 'best' marker, plus one pictogram hint chosen from what cost you most (for example the [DOWN] then [UP] keycaps if you never rebounded). Input is accepted from 0.6 s. Any key: a reverse whoosh, night wipes back across the frame in 0.4 s, and you are grinding the opening rail with the dawn 14 m behind you and the kick restarting on the next beat. Death to control is about 1.0 s. On retries the 4 s dawn-break beat is cut and the keycap prompts are hidden; the rest of sector 1 is drawn from the seeded S1 pattern pool and still ends in the CATHEDRAL, so the big-air moment opens every run but the route to it changes.

## Bot strategy
The whole game is analysable in closed form: rails are segments in four tiers, beacons are points, hazards are boxes or circles on a beat clock, a swing is a circular arc with a fixed angular profile, and every flight is a parabola (or a straight dive) with a closed-form landing solver, the same solver that draws the ghost arc and drives aim assist. The sim runs at a fixed 120 Hz, is seeded, and its state is small enough to clone. POLICY (greedy with a 2 s look-ahead): at each decision point (touching a rail, leaving a rail, a beacon entering range, the apex of a flight, a hazard entering the look-ahead) enumerate at most six macro-actions: ride on; jump; rebound; drop-through; latch and release at angle A (A sampled at 30, 40 and 48 deg); dive now or at the first frame that lands on the best charged rail. Forward-simulate each on a cloned state to the next stable contact and score it: +3 per pip, +2 for ending on a rail, a small bonus for a higher tier, -4 for a LATE, -10 per floor touch, -12 per burn; ties go to the fewest inputs. Execute the winner and repeat. The rules of thumb that fall out: always hold UP when a beacon is ahead; release in the last third of the sweet zone; dive whenever the solver says the dive lands on a charged rail; rebound after every slam unless a lightfall is overhead; drop a tier when a lightfall covers yours; stomp any warden that sits under the arc. THREE PRESETS from one bot: PERFECT (frame-exact, takes whipcracks) must reach sector 9 on any seed, which proves every generated layout is solvable; NAIVE (jumps at gaps only, never slings) must die between 60 and 80 s, which proves the difficulty floor; ATTRACT (a deterministic 50 to 90 ms reaction delay, releases mid-zone, never whipcracks) plays the title screen and looks like a good human. Each pattern is validated headlessly at its native gear and one gear either side before it is allowed into the pool.

## Tech notes
SIMULATION: fixed 120 Hz step with render interpolation, seeded PRNG, world in metres, patterns as plain data authored in beats. Collision is trivial by design: the runner is a feet point swept against rail segments (segment-segment test, 0.6 m magnet, 0.5 m end forgiveness) and a 0.6 x 1.6 m box against hazard boxes and warden circles, with hitboxes 20% smaller than the art. Swings are analytic (an angle advanced along a fixed profile, no rope physics) and flights are analytic parabolas. Gameplay never reads audio time: the beat clock is sim time, and the audio scheduler is aligned to it at run start and nudged at bar lines. RENDERING (WebGL2): one dynamic quad batch using a small generated atlas (soft dot, soft line, ring, hard quad, font glyphs); lines are quads with soft edges; alpha and additive batches per layer, around ten draw calls for the scene. Far layers render to a half-res target. The scene goes to an RGBA16F target (RGBA8 fallback), then a bright-pass, a 5-level half-res downsample-upsample bloom, and one composite pass doing distortion (8 ripple uniforms plus the dawn haze band), radial chromatic aberration, scanlines, vignette, grain and the dawn overexposure, followed by a crisp pass that redraws rail cores, hazard outlines, the runner and the HUD over the result. CHEAP: lines, glow sprites, 4,000 pooled CPU particles, the scarf ribbon (40 points), rail string waves (16 vertices per rail), forward shadows (skewed quads), skyline (generated once per chunk into reusable vertex data), holo-creatures (a few hundred line segments each). EXPENSIVE, so budgeted: full-screen additive overdraw (at most three screen-sized glow quads at once), bloom at full resolution (never), ember particles from the burning skyline (capped at 600). AUDIO: one context; a look-ahead scheduler (25 ms tick, 120 ms horizon) driving a 16-step sequencer; every music layer always playing into its own gain node; a master chain of low-pass, compressor and convolver send; sidechain done as scheduled gain dips; a pool of 16 one-shot voices built from oscillators and shared noise buffers, pitched from the current chord table. The same deterministic sim runs headless for bot validation.

## Biggest risk
The feel of the sling. If release timing reads as arbitrary, new players spend the run bouncing off the floor in gear 1, and a no-death design turns that into the worst outcome a flow game can have: a slow, boring failure. Mitigations, in order of importance: 1) the swing is kinematic and rhythmic (always about one beat), so the timing can be learned by ear as well as by eye; 2) the release is communicated on three channels at once: tether colour, riser pitch with a 'ting', and the live ghost arc that turns lime when letting go will land; 3) the GOOD zone is wide (about 230 ms) and aim-assisted so it always arrives, while the skill expression lives in the narrower SLING and WHIPCRACK zones stacked on top of it; 4) sector 1 can be completed with jumps and tier hops alone, the floor always has an on-ramp within 2 beats, and a skid-rebound reaches tier 2, so recovery is always one action away; 5) CLUTCH doubles pips near the dawn, so the endgame is a fast last stand rather than a slow fade; 6) the tuning numbers (angular profile, zone angles, rope ratio, launch speeds) are data, and the three-preset bot plus the headless validator show immediately when a change makes a pattern unfair. The secondary risk is neon overload; the hue, shape and value laws and the post-bloom crisp pass exist to prevent it.

## Why this wins
It passes the toy test: with every hazard removed, jump, sling, slam and rebound over a field of rails and beacons is already fun, because each verb hands you the momentum the next one needs and each has a physical wind-up and a release the whole screen answers. That is two keys, UP and DOWN, with obvious meanings, learned in the first twenty seconds from three pictograms. On top of the toy sits one system instead of five: gear is health, score multiplier, camera, colour and soundtrack at once, so playing well is immediately louder, wider and brighter, and playing badly is a legible slide rather than a cut to a game-over screen. Mistakes cost momentum before they cost the run, which keeps the player in motion and makes the near-death comeback the most exciting state in the game. The dawn gives the genre's abstract 'go right' an image anyone understands in two seconds, a diegetic health bar, a colour law that solves neon readability (warm hurts, everything else is night), and an ending that is always the player's own doing. It is also the right shape for this build: four tiers, segments, points and boxes, analytic arcs, beat-authored patterns that are valid in every gear, a bot that proves each layout, and visuals made entirely of what a line-and-glow batch renderer does best. And the first thirty seconds play like a film: dawn breaks, the first swing goes through a hologram, the first slam shifts the music up, and the big-air reveal of the whole ring ends in a drop that lands on the player's own input.

## Juice
- Hitstop on every landed skill: 40 ms SLING, 70 ms WHIPCRACK, 50 ms SLAM, 30 ms STOMP, 90 ms burn (the runner flashes white-orange). The sim freezes; particles and audio keep running.
- Camera as speedometer: zoom eases 1.00 to 0.72 with gear, rolls 2.5 deg into swings, kicks 10 px on slams (spring, 12 Hz, damping 0.5), punches in 3% and releases on flings, and pulls to 0.60 for big air. Shake is trauma-based (trauma squared) and reserved for burns and dawn proximity, so it always means something.
- Rails are strings: every jump, landing and slam sends a visible travelling wave along the rail (0.3 to 0.6 m amplitude, 60 m/s, decaying) and plays a bass note from the current chord. A slam visibly bows the rail, and a rebound is the rail firing you back.
- The scarf is the flow meter: a 40-point ribbon whose length and colour are the gear (cyan, blue, violet, magenta, white with RGB fringing), whose segments light as pips land, and which doubles in width during a swing. The last 3 s of your path stay drawn in the sky as a fading afterglow, so you can see the line you drew.
- Screen-space shockwaves: slams, flings, stomps and gear shifts push a ripple ring through the distortion pass; the dawn front carries a permanent heat-haze band.
- Gear shift up: 100 ms at 0.85x time, a mechanical 'clunk', then speed surges over 0.4 s with radial speed lines, a zoom-out, +10% bloom and saturation, the gear numeral punching to 160% and settling, and the new music layer unmuting on a stinger. Gear 5 turns the trail white and deepens the scanlines.
- Gear shift down: the top music layer dies in a 150 ms tape-stop (pitch dips 2 semitones), the master low-passes to 700 Hz and recovers over 2 beats, the scarf snaps shorter, saturation drops and a hot vignette pulses in from the left.
- Squash and stretch on the 14-primitive runner: 1.25 x 0.75 for 2 frames on a jump, 0.8 x 1.3 in flight, a spear pose with three afterimages in a dive, a 3-frame crouch on landing. Inputs change the pose and the velocity on the same frame; anticipation is never placed before the response.
- Chromatic aberration tied to speed (0.5 px in gear 1 to 2.5 px in gear 5, radial, zero in the look-ahead corridor) with 6 px spikes on flings and burns that decay in 200 ms.
- The world keeps time: beacons, rail edge lights and skyline windows pulse on the kick (scale 1.0 to 1.08, fast decay); wardens bob and lightfalls pulse on the beat clock; from gear 3 the whole skyline breathes with the sidechain.
- The tether has a temperature: lime to white through the swing under a climbing riser, a 'ting' and a runner flash when the sweet zone opens, a snap-and-recoil animation on release, and a ghost arc that turns lime, then white.
- Forward shadows: when the dawn is within 15 m the runner, rails and wardens throw long skewed shadows to the right that lengthen as it closes, and the left edge blooms toward white. Within 6 m the mix band-passes and an alarm pulses a minor second.
- Things break beautifully: wardens pop into triangle shards with a note that climbs F minor pentatonic per consecutive stomp; holo-signs glitch and shatter around your arc; towers caught by the dawn crack into embers that blow forward past you.
- Grind sparks scale with speed and take the gear colour; on the hull they turn grey and the sound becomes a gritty scrape, so the slow lane feels like running in sand.
- Death is one continuous gesture: 350 ms of slow motion as the light reaches you, the silhouette goes black against white and blows apart as ash, the frame blooms out, the music collapses to a single held sub note, and the first key press wipes night back across the screen.
