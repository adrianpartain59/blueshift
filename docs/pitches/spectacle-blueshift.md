# BLUESHIFT  (pitch:spectacle)

**Hook:** You outrun a supernova down the hollow middle of a neon space city that wraps all the way around you, and you never jump: you spin the whole tunnel, flip gravity through its core, and punch through firewalls on the beat, and every wall you break makes the world bluer and faster.

**Fantasy:** You are the last courier out of the Spindle, a cylinder megacity whose star has just gone nova. You ride a mag-board down the city's hollow axis, eight rails of conduit lined with still-armed security firewalls, toward a jump gate glowing at the vanishing point. The shockfront is seconds behind you and gaining, and speed is the only health bar you have.

**Perspective:** Third-person chase camera inside an octagonal tube: 8 lanes, apothem 5 m, lane width 4.1 m. The camera sits 8 m behind and 2.4 m above the runner looking straight down the axis; the runner is a crouched mag-board silhouette about 12% of screen height, three-quarters of the way down the screen, and the world rolls so the runner's lane is always 'down'. Vertical FOV is 68 deg at Gear 1 and widens to 92 deg at Gear 5. The 3D pipeline is deliberately tiny: every object lives in tube space (lane angle, radius, time-to-arrival x current speed), is moved to view space (roll angle, camera offset, and a curved-world bend x,y += k*z^2 that makes the tunnel snake and dive for free), near-plane clipped, perspective-divided and emitted as screen-space quads. Lines are quads whose width scales with 1/z; solids are near-black filled polygons with thick neon rims; glow is additive sprites. Depth is sold by exponential fog, octagon ribs every 8 m, and four parallax shells: the play tube (r 5 m), a service lattice of gantries and glyph signs (r 9-12 m), sky-traffic light trains (r 15-40 m) and the megacity itself wrapped 360 degrees around you (r 60-140 m, wire-prism towers pointing inward, window-dot grids, holo billboards). The jump gate is a small bright ring at the vanishing point. The nova is never drawn ahead of you: it exists only as gold light, haze and embers arriving from behind and from the screen edges.

**Core loop:** Second to second: read the next 2-4 beats coming up the tube, then answer each hazard with the one axis that beats it. Go AROUND magenta solids (roll), ACROSS through the open core (flip), or THROUGH yellow ICE (breach on the beat). Clean, late, close answers pay FLUX; FLUX shifts you up a gear; each gear is faster, bluer, louder and multiplies score, but gives you less warning. Crashes never kill outright: they drop two gears and let the nova gain. Minute to minute: the nova's speed steps up every 16-bar sector, so the gear that was safe thirty seconds ago is now losing ground and you must take the greedier line to stay ahead. Run to run: the nova always wins eventually; the question is how far you got and how hot you ran. Retry is 1.3 s and drops you straight back into the drop, so 'one more' means chasing the next sector reveal, a longer PERFECT chain, and a tighter graze line through patterns you now recognise.

## Controls
- **Left / Right (arrows or A / D; touch: tap left or right third of the screen)** → ROLL one lane around the tube. The logical lane changes on the same frame; holding auto-repeats after 160 ms at one lane per 85 ms. A roll into a lane occupied by a long fin is refused and becomes a wall-grind instead of a crash.  
  _Feedback:_ The entire world snaps 45 degrees around you in 90 ms with a 6-degree overshoot and settle while the runner stays bottom-centre; the runner banks 30 degrees and its two light ribbons whip; cyan sparks scrape off the rail you land on; a plucked synth note plays, one scale step up for right and down for left (8 lanes = one octave of F minor), panned toward the move, with a small chromatic-aberration tick.
- **Up (arrow or W; touch: swipe up)** → FLIP: leap off your lane, pass through the tube's core and land on the opposite lane (lane+4). Airtime is 0.75 beat (352 ms at 128 BPM). One Left/Right tap in mid-air shifts the landing lane by one; Space works in mid-air.  
  _Feedback:_ The world rotates a full 180 degrees around the fixed vanishing point with ease-in-out while the runner corkscrews up through screen centre and back down, its ribbons drawing a helix that lingers for 0.5 s; radial blur ramps up; a sub 'whoomp' (110 to 45 Hz) with a reversed noise swell. Landing gives squash-and-stretch (0.7 > 1.1 > 1.0 in 160 ms), a ring of sparks racing up the landing lane, a 0.2 m camera dip and a thud. A reticle on the opposite lane shows where you will land and turns magenta if it is blocked.
- **Space (or J / X; touch: tap centre third)** → BREACH: a 180 ms ram state that shatters yellow ICE on contact. A hit cancels the recovery so stacked walls can be drummed; a whiff costs a 200 ms recovery.  
  _Feedback:_ A yellow-white lance cone snaps out ahead of the board, FOV kicks +14 degrees and a bolt of light fires down your rail to the vanishing point with a punchy 'thoom', even on a whiff. On a hit: 60-90 ms visual freeze, the wall bursts into about 120 hex shards that rake past the camera, a shockwave ring distorts the screen, every rib ahead flashes in sequence as a light pulse races into the distance, and a kick + glass-noise burst + in-key chord stab lands exactly on the beat with PERFECT or GOOD stamped into the flying shards.

## Mechanics
### AROUND: Roll, Graze and Grind
- Rules: The tube has 9 cells: 8 lanes plus the core. Every hazard is one of two materials (SOLID magenta or ICE yellow) times a 9-bit cell mask, arriving on a 16th-note tick. On the ground you occupy one lane cell; if a SOLID's mask contains your cell at its arrival tick, you crash. Roll changes your cell instantly (the 90 ms spin is cosmetic), at most one lane per 85 ms, with a 110 ms input buffer. GRAZE: passing a solid in an adjacent lane pays +5 FLUX per side; both sides on the same tick is a THREAD, +12. Fins are solids 2-16 beats long: a roll into a fin is refused with no damage, holding against it GRINDS for +5 FLUX per beat, and the held roll fires the instant that lane frees up.
- Why fun: Lateral movement is the spectacle: you are not sliding a sprite, you are spinning a city. Because rolls are instant and unlimited the player feels total control at absurd speed, and the graze rule turns every dodge into the question of how close you dare cut it. Holding against a helical fin and drilling the whole world round with sparks pouring off the rail is this game's version of a Thumper turn.
- Interplay: Where you stand decides what the other two verbs do: a flip lands exactly opposite, so rolling is how you aim it; plated ICE can only be breached from a bare lane, so you roll and then strike. Grazes are the steady FLUX income between the big breach payouts, and grinding a fin commits you to whatever is waiting where the fin ends.
### ACROSS: Gravity Flip through the core
- Rules: A flip lasts 0.75 beat. From 0.08 to 0.67 beat you occupy the core cell: immune to everything on the lanes (blocks, fins, irises), but SOLID core cells (spokes, core mines) crash you and ICE in the core must be breached in mid-air. You land on lane+4 (or +3 / +5 with one mid-air roll tap); landing on a solid crashes. Irises (all 8 lanes solid, core open) always sit on the off-beat with a cyan launch pad on the beat before, so the correct press is ON the beat: the window is -80 ms to +197 ms at 128 BPM. Passing an iris pays +10 FLUX; a pad press within 50 ms of the beat is CLEAN, +16. Core orbs are collected automatically while you are in the core, +4 each.
- Why fun: It is the one move a side-scroller cannot have: the depth axis itself becomes a place you can go. The whole city turns upside down in a third of a second, you thread the hole in a wall at 80 m/s, and you slam down on what used to be the ceiling. It is big, legible and slightly scary every single time.
- Interplay: Flip is the only answer to an iris and the fastest way across the tube (four lanes in one input), but it commits you for three-quarters of a beat and to a lane you had to read at the top of the screen first. Orbs in the core make it a greed move over hazards you could have rolled around; spokes and core mines make it the wrong answer exactly when it has become a habit. An iris with ICE in its hole demands flip, then breach in mid-air.
### THROUGH: Beat Breach
- Rules: ICE walls cover all 9 cells and arrive only on beats. Space opens a 180 ms ram; the wall breaks if it arrives during the ram, or if you press up to 60 ms after it arrives (late grace). Press window: T-180 ms to T+60 ms. PERFECT is a press within 50 ms of the beat: +30 FLUX, plus +4 for each consecutive PERFECT (cap +20). GOOD is +18. A hit cancels recovery, so walls stacked on 8th notes can be drummed; a whiff costs 200 ms of recovery, -3 FLUX and the chain, so mashing fails roughly half the time. Every wall carries three concentric rings that collapse on the three beats before impact with rising pips. Ramming a SOLID does nothing: you crash. Each 16-bar sector ends in a double-thick gate that grants +1 gear and +30 m of lead when breached.
- Why fun: This is the percussion instrument. The backing track mutes its snare in ICE phrases and its entire drum kit in the fill bar, so the player's breaches are the missing hits. Smashing a wall on the kick with shards flying past the lens is the most satisfying button in the game, and pressing later is both better music and better score.
- Interplay: Breach is useless against magenta and mandatory against yellow, which splits the player's attention cleanly by colour. ICE is a see-through lattice, so a slab placed one beat behind it is readable to the careful and a trap for the careless. Plated ICE needs a roll first, an iris lock needs a flip first. Breaches are the main FLUX source, buying the speed that everything else is paid in.
### BLUESHIFT vs. the NOVA: speed is life
- Rules: FLUX fills a ring; 60 / 80 / 100 / 120 FLUX shifts Gear 1 > 2 > 3 > 4 > 5. Gear speeds are 48 / 60 / 72 / 86 / 100 m/s and the score multiplier is x1 to x5. Hazards are time-locked to the music, so a higher gear never changes the rhythm; it shortens warning time (hazards materialise 150 m out: 3.1 s at Gear 1, 1.5 s at Gear 5). LEAD is your distance to the shockfront: starts at 220 m, caps at 300 m, and changes by (gear speed - nova speed) every second. Nova speed by sector: 36, 50, 64, 78, 90, 98, then +4 per sector. A crash costs 2 gears (minimum Gear 1), all FLUX and 40 m of LEAD, and gives 1.2 s of ghost invulnerability. At Gear 5, overflow FLUX converts to LEAD at 0.5 m per point. LEAD 0 means caught.
- Why fun: It is one number you feel instead of read: fall behind and gold light, heat haze and overtaking embers crawl in from the screen edges; pull ahead and the world goes cold, blue and fast. Novices get a forgiving health bar, experts get a reason to redline, and every crash has an immediate dramatic consequence and a comeback arc.
- Interplay: It is the single economy that all three verbs feed. It also self-balances: a crash slows you, which doubles your warning time exactly when you are rattled, while clean play pushes you into gears where reading is hard. The nova's sector steps eventually force every player onto the greedy line.

## First 30 seconds
- 0-2s: IGNITION. Any key: the title logo shatters into hex shards that fly past the lens and the camera drops behind the courier, clamped in a dark launch barrel. Three ribs ignite toward the vanishing point, one per beat, with rising pips, then a reverse-cymbal riser. Rolling is live from the first frame: tap left or right and the barrel snaps 45 degrees with a pluck. At 1.9 s the board fires and auto-rams the bay shutter on the downbeat: freeze-frame, yellow shards rake the camera, a light pulse races down the ribs, kick and filtered bass come in at 128 BPM. Before pressing anything useful the player has seen exactly what yellow walls are for.
- 2-5s: AROUND. Closed violet conduit, 48 m/s. A magenta slab is coming up the player's own lane, its telegraph stripe running down the floor toward them, two arrow glyphs pulsing beside the runner. Slab one lands at 3.8 s, slab two at 4.7 s; both are aimed at whatever lane the player is standing in, and a curl of cyan sparks shows the way out. Each roll spins the conduit and plays a note; each dodged slab whooshes past the lens. A hit here is only a stumble: nothing can kill yet.
- 5-7s: THROUGH. A yellow hex wall has been counting itself in behind the slabs: three rings collapse on three beats, pip, pip, pip, with a space-bar glyph pulsing over the runner. 5.6 s, bar-3 downbeat, window widened to +/-200 ms for this first one: Space, lance, FOV punch, 60 ms freeze, 120 shards, shockwave, and the hit lands as the snare. One beat later two slabs flank the lane the player is already in: a free THREAD, sparks off both shoulders, the FLUX ring jumps.
- 7-9s: THE BUILD. Snare roll and riser. One last aimed slab at 7.5 s, then three beats of empty conduit while the end shutter fills the screen: a double-thick yellow disc stamped with a giant glyph, counting 3-2-1. Wall panels flicker, warning glyphs scroll, and a gold light that belongs to nobody's palette starts creeping up the ribs from behind the camera.
- 9-10s: THE DROP. 9.4 s: breach the shutter (miss it and you crash through anyway; the reveal is never gated on skill). The tunnel is gone. You are flying down the hollow axis of a cylinder megacity with towers pointing at you from every direction, and in the same beat the nova ignites behind the lens: gold rim-light and long shadows snap forward along every rail, the bassline drops, GEAR 2 stamps across the screen, the track ahead telescopes, and the LEAD arc slides in under the runner.
- 10-15s: CORKSCREW. Two fins form a helical channel that turns one lane per beat for ten beats, with sparks laid down its tight edge. Hold Right: the board grinds the leading fin in a fountain of sparks while the entire city drills 450 degrees around you in beat-locked clicks. One key held, maximum spectacle: the game is showing off and the player feels brilliant.
- 15-17s: GEAR UP. ICE wall on the bar-8 downbeat; for most players this breach tips the FLUX ring into GEAR 3: the world shifts from indigo to azure, the arp layer enters, the tunnel stretches away and rushes back. Then a real thread gate two lanes over, read from the stripes on the floor.
- 17-22s: ACROSS. A magenta ring with a cyan-rimmed hole and an orb floating in it, a chevron launch pad on the lane before it and an up-arrow glyph over the runner. Flip on the beat: the city turns upside down around the vanishing point, the orb chimes mid-core, the board slams onto what was the ceiling. A second iris one bar later flips you back, then a four-beat slalom along the spark line, grazing every slab.
- 22-30s: FIRST PHRASE. The three verbs chained for the first time, one input per beat and nothing new: ICE with a slab hiding one beat behind the lattice (breach, roll), iris into ICE (flip, land, breach), a thread gate, a slalom. Gold embers begin overtaking the camera if the player has been crashing; the world runs colder and faster if they have not.
- 30-32s: THE FILL. Bar 16: the drums cut out and four ICE walls arrive on four beats. Smash, smash, smash, smash: the player is the snare fill, straight into the SECTOR 02 gate on the downbeat for a guaranteed gear-up as the tempo lifts to 132. Thirty-two seconds, every mechanic used, not one word read.

## Signature moment
The Drop, 9.4 seconds in. The riser cuts to silence for one 16th, you hit Space, and the conduit's end shutter, a 10 m disc of yellow hex-ICE stamped with a giant glyph, explodes into shards. Behind it there is no tunnel: you are flying down the hollow axis of a cylinder city, neon towers pointing at you from every direction, and in the same frame the nova ignites behind the camera, throwing gold rim-light down every tower and long shadows forward along the rails as the bassline lands and a pulse of light races away from you down the ribs toward the jump gate. The screenshot: yellow shards mid-air, a city wrapped all the way around the frame, gold bleeding in at the edges, one cyan runner, one ring of light halfway to the vanishing point. The 5-second clip continues into the corkscrew, with the whole city drilling around the board.

## Hazards and patterns
Model: 9 cells (8 lanes + core) x 2 materials on a 16th-note grid; every hazard is (arrival tick, material, cell mask, length). Vocabulary: BLOCK (magenta slab, 1-3 lanes, one tick): roll around it. FIN (magenta wall, one lane, 2-16 beats): corridor edge, grind it. IRIS (magenta annulus, all 8 lanes, core open, on the off-beat with a launch pad on the beat before): flip. SPOKE (magenta bar: two opposite lanes + core) and CORE MINE (core only): punish flips. ICE WALL (yellow hex membrane, all 9 cells, on the beat): breach. ICE PYLON (yellow, one lane): optional breach for a full payout. PLATED ICE (ICE wall + magenta plates on some lanes): roll to a bare lane, then breach. IRIS LOCK (iris + ICE in the hole): flip, breach in mid-air. Pickups: SPARKS (lane pips tracing the high-value line), ORBS (core only). Patterns, with L = the runner's lane at pattern start; the generator mirrors and rotates all of them: (1) SLALOM, 1 bar: blocks {L, L+1} on beat 1, {L-1, L-2} on beat 2, repeat. The tight answer L-1, L, L-1, L grazes every beat; the lazy answer is to leave for L+3 and earn nothing. (2) CORKSCREW, 3-4 bars: two fins bound a 2-lane channel that steps one lane per beat for 10-12 beats; hold toward the leading fin to grind while the city drills around you; ends in an ICE wall on the downbeat. Later tiers reverse direction mid-way and seed ICE pylons in the channel. (3) THREAD GATE: seven lanes blocked, one open at L+/-2, telegraphed 4 beats out (2 beats at high tiers); a guaranteed THREAD; doubles appear on opposite sides one bar apart. (4) IRIS LADDER: irises on beats 1 and 3 (tier 1) or 1, 2, 3 (tier 3), an orb in every hole; at tier 3 a block hits the final landing lane half a beat after touchdown, so steer in mid-air or roll instantly. (5) BLIND BREACH: ICE wall on beat 1 with a block in your lane on beat 2 (beat 1.5 at tier 3), visible only as a magenta shadow through the lattice. (6) PLATED ICE: wall with plates on {L-1, L, L+1}; at tier 3 only two adjacent lanes are bare, three lanes away. (7) PROPELLER, 2-4 bars: a spoke on every beat, rotating one lane per beat; walk one lane ahead of the blade for a graze per beat, or step back through it between beats; no flips, the core is blocked; tier 3 uses a four-armed cross. (8) ORB BAIT: off-beat half-rings alternately block the five lanes under you and the five opposite, with an orb in the core each time; flip on every beat to harvest (or scramble two lanes per beat for nothing); the fourth half-ring hides a core mine, so that one must be rolled. (9) IRIS LOCK: flip on the beat, breach on the '&'; at tier 4 you land straight into a thread gate. (10) DRUM FILL, the last bar of every sector: ICE walls on beats 1-2-3-4 (8th notes in the second half at tier 3) with the backing drums muted, then the double-thick SECTOR GATE on the downbeat. Assembly: a sector is 16 bars, 5-7 patterns drawn from the current tier pool with 1-2 beats of air between them, never two new ideas at once, always ending in the fill and the gate.

## Readability
Six hard rules. (1) Reserved space: nothing decorative ever enters the play tube (r < 5 m); all decoration lives at r >= 9 m. (2) Fill rule: solids are the only dark-filled shapes in the game, near-black polygons with a thick saturated rim, so they read as holes punched in the glow at any speed; decoration is hairlines only, capped at 35% luminance; ICE is the only full-disc lattice. (3) Reserved colours: magenta = solid, never touch; yellow = ICE, hit Space (the breach lance is the same yellow); cyan and white = you, pickups, launch pads and safe guidance; orange-gold = the nova, and only ever at the screen edges. Decoration is restricted to the violet-to-ice-blue gear ramp. Shape backs colour for colour-blind players: solids are chevron-striped slabs, ICE is a hex lattice with three countdown rings, an iris has a cyan-rimmed hole. (4) Lane telegraphs: every solid projects a magenta stripe one beat long down its lane toward you, and your own lane's cyan guide line turns magenta between you and the next solid in it, so 'am I on a collision course' is one glance at the floor, not a 3D judgment. (5) Strike ring and beat-lock: a brighter octagon rib marks the runner's depth; hazards arrive only on the 16th grid, every required press is on a beat, ICE counts itself in 3-2-1, irises have launch pads. (6) FX budget: bloom thresholds so only rims bloom; aberration, distortion and blur are zero at screen centre, where the future is, and grow toward the edges; hit effects decay within 250 ms, less than one beat; hazards more than two lanes from you render at 55% so the near threat pops. The HUD is three things: the LEAD arc under the runner, the gear numeral with its FLUX ring at top centre, and the score at top right.

## Risk / reward
Score = metres travelled x gear, plus event points x gear: PERFECT breach 500, GOOD 200, CLEAN flip 300, iris pass 150, THREAD 250, graze 50, orb 40. Four greed loops. (1) Late is great: pressing early on ICE is always safe because the ram lasts 180 ms, but only the on-beat press is PERFECT and builds the chain bonus. (2) Hug or clear: the lane two away from a block is free and worthless; the lane beside it pays, and the single-lane THREAD gap pays most. Holding against a fin grinds FLUX every beat, but the held roll fires the instant the fin ends, into whatever is there. (3) Core harvest: orbs float in the core above hazards you could simply roll around; taking them means a flip onto a lane you had to read at the top of the screen, and orb lines sometimes end in a core mine. (4) Optional ICE: single-lane yellow pylons can be dodged for nothing or breached for a full breach payout, at the cost of standing in their lane. Everything converts to the same currency, gear, which multiplies score and outruns the nova but halves your warning time. The best players hold Gear 5 with LEAD pinned at the cap and convert overflow FLUX into extra metres; the leaderboard number is distance x heat.

## Escalation
16-bar sectors; tempo rises 4 BPM per sector. 0:00-0:09 CONDUIT (128 BPM, Gear 1 at 48 m/s, one event every 2-4 beats, no nova, crashes cost nothing). 0:09-0:32 the Drop into THE SPINDLE: nova ignites at 36 m/s; rolls, corkscrew, first ICE phrases and irises, events every 1-2 beats; expect Gear 2-3. 0:32-1:01 Sector 2 (132 BPM, nova 50 m/s): iris ladders, blind breaches, thread gates; Gear 1 now loses ground. 1:01-1:29 Sector 3 FOUNDRY (136 BPM, nova 64): propellers, plated ICE, core mines, first 8th-note blocks; you need Gear 3 (72 m/s). 1:29-1:57 Sector 4 SHATTER (140 BPM, nova 78): iris locks, switchback landings, reversing corkscrews, the city breaking apart in gold light; you need Gear 4 (86 m/s). 1:57-2:23 Sector 5 CORONA (144 BPM, nova 90): three-verb phrases every bar, 8th-note fills, bare rails over the star's limb; you need Gear 5 (100 m/s). 2:23 onward FLARE sectors (148 BPM cap, nova 98 then +4 per sector): all pools at maximum density; from the second FLARE sector even Gear 5 bleeds lead, so the run ends when banked LEAD and perfect-play overflow run out. Tuning targets: a first-timer who crashes every 6 seconds still survives about a minute and typically dies in Sector 2-3 (60-100 s); a competent player dies in Sector 4-5 (2 to 2.5 min); experts push past 4 minutes.

## Audio direction
128 BPM rising 4 per sector to a 148 cap; F minor, 4-bar loop Fm - Db - Ab - Eb. A real-time sequencer runs on the AudioContext clock, which is also the game clock. Voices: sine-drop kick, noise + tone snare, high-passed noise hats, saw bass through an enveloped low-pass with sidechain ducking, square/saw pluck arp with ping-pong delay, detuned-saw pad, supersaw lead and stabs, noise risers, all through a master compressor and soft clip. Layers follow gear: Gear 1 kick + closed hats + 8th-note sub pulse; Gear 2 adds offbeat open hat, clap and a rolling 16th bass; Gear 3 adds the arp; Gear 4 adds the sidechained pad and a counter-melody; Gear 5 adds supersaw stabs, an octave-up arp, a ride and a fully open filter. The player is in the band: roll plucks step through the scale (8 lanes = 8 scale degrees), sparks play the arp, ICE phrases mute the snare so breaches supply it, and the last bar of every sector drops the drums entirely so four breaches are the fill. Key SFX: breach = 160 > 40 Hz punch + band-swept glass noise + chord stab in the current chord (PERFECT adds an octave sparkle run); whiffed breach = the punch alone; flip = sub whoomp + reverse swell, landing thud; graze = Doppler zzip; hazard fly-by whooshes panned by lane; gear-up = snare roll + riser + 4-note rise; crash = tape-stop detune + distorted noise hit; low LEAD = Geiger hats, two-tone klaxon on the beat, Shepard riser; death = hard cut, reverse swell, sub boom.

## Failure and restart
Hitting things never kills directly: a crash is a stumble (2 gears, 40 m of LEAD, 1.2 s as a ghost). The only death is the nova catching you at LEAD 0, usually on the crash that spends your last 40 m, so the cause is always legible. Death beat: 0.4 s white-gold wipe from the screen edges, the runner dissolves forward into particles, the music hard-cuts to a sub boom, and DISTANCE / SECTOR / BEST burn into the white. Input is accepted after 0.3 s; any key collapses the white into the launch shutter, a 2-beat quick-launch (0.94 s) auto-rams it on the downbeat and you are already in the Spindle at Gear 2 with the bassline in. That is 1.3 s from death to control, and retries skip the 9-second Conduit and every prompt glyph, so each restart opens on the game's best moment. From the title: any key, a one-bar ignition of 1.9 s with rolling live from the first frame. Six idle seconds on the title or results screen starts bot-driven attract mode. Best distance and best sector persist locally.

## Bot strategy
The whole game is a table: hazards are (arrival tick, material, 9-bit cell mask, length) and the runner is (lane 0-7, mode ground / core / landing, ram timer). The bot looks 8 beats ahead and runs dynamic programming over hazard ticks with state (lane, airborne or not, ram ready or not). Transitions are 'roll n lanes' (legal if n x 85 ms fits before the tick and no fin blocks the way), 'flip on the preceding beat' and 'breach'. It discards any state whose cell is in a SOLID mask, or in an ICE mask without an active ram, then picks the surviving path with the highest FLUX: graze-adjacent lanes, orbs, PERFECT timing. Execution is frame-exact on the audio clock: breach presses at T-10 ms, flips on the pad beat, rolls as late as the margin allows. A skill parameter adds Gaussian timing jitter and a reaction delay for a human-looking attract mode and for simulating novice run lengths. With zero jitter the same solver is the level validator: it asserts that every generated sector is clearable at every gear with at least 150 ms of slack per required input and at least 2 beats of visibility, and it replays fixed seeds as regression tests. The generator is seeded, so any failing run is reproducible.

## Tech notes
Render: one dynamic vertex buffer of coloured 2D triangles built on the CPU each frame (tube space to view space, near-plane clip, project, expand lines to quads), roughly 40-60k vertices. Painter's order by time bucket, far to near: alpha-blended dark fills first, then additive rims, glows and particles. No depth buffer, no meshes, no textures except the generated font atlas and one radial glow sprite. Post chain: scene FBO > bright-pass > separable blur at 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8 resolution > one composite shader for bloom add, radial chromatic aberration, barrel + shockwave-ring + edge heat-haze distortion, crash scanline tear, nova edge glow, scanlines, grain and vignette. Collision is not geometric at all: it is the cell-mask lookup at hazard ticks, so it is exact, frame-rate independent and free. Clock: AudioContext.currentTime drives a fixed 120 Hz logic step; music is scheduled 100 ms ahead; visuals compensate for output latency; hit-stop is a decaying visual time offset, never a real pause. Cheap: ribs, rails, lattice walls, starfield, city prisms, and every camera trick (roll, FOV, bend and telescoping are a uniform or one multiply). Expensive and therefore capped: additive overdraw from shards and glows near the lens (clamp sprite size and alpha, about 2,500 particles maximum), the full-resolution composite pass (all blur taps stay at reduced resolution), and per-frame CPU line expansion for the city (about 150 towers, culled by fog). Patterns are plain data arrays of [tick, material, mask, length] that the generator mirrors and rotates for variety.

## Biggest risk
Depth timing plus sensory overload. Into-the-screen games make 'when exactly does that reach me' hard to judge from perspective alone, and here the camera also rolls, flips and blooms. If a first-timer feels the crashes are unreadable, the pitch collapses into a pretty screensaver. The mitigation is structural, not cosmetic: the tube is treated as a rhythm highway. Hazards land only on the beat grid at a visible strike ring, every required press is on a beat, ICE counts itself in 3-2-1 and irises have launch pads, so timing is heard as much as seen. Windows are wide (240 ms for breach including late grace, 277 ms for flip). Lane telegraph stripes answer 'which lane' on the floor instead of in 3D. Solids are the only filled shapes. Post effects are zero at screen centre and shorter than one beat. Rolls and flips pivot about a vanishing point that never moves, which is also the main defence against motion sickness. Crashes are non-lethal and the first 9 seconds cannot kill you. The validator bot proves every generated sector is clearable with slack at every gear, and a comfort toggle halves roll speed, shake and aberration.

## Why this wins
Speed sells itself in two seconds, and this concept gives it three verbs that only exist because of depth: around the tube, across its core, through its walls. Each verb maps to one colour, one shape and one key, each has an enormous audiovisual payoff even on a whiff, and all three feed a single economy, speed, which is also your health bar against a threat with real fiction behind it. The drop at 9.4 seconds (wall shatters, city wraps around you, star explodes behind you, bass lands) is a guaranteed 'whoa' that needs no skill to reach and replays on every retry. Beat-locking makes the player the drummer, so it feels good in the hands even before it is understood. And it is unusually buildable to a high polish: a 9-cell by 16th-note table instead of physics, lines and flat polygons instead of art, and one solver that serves as attract mode, test harness and fairness proof. It is tight, loud and legible, and it could only be an into-the-screen game.

## Juice
- Roll: 45-degree world snap in 90 ms with spring overshoot, runner bank, ribbon whip, rail sparks, and a pitched pluck per lane so movement plays the scale.
- Breach hit: 60 ms (GOOD) or 90 ms (PERFECT) visual hit-stop that then catches up over 120 ms so the audio clock never pauses; +14 deg FOV punch; about 120 hex shards with outward and toward-camera velocity; expanding shockwave distortion ring; one-frame 20% white flash; chromatic split at full strength decaying over 300 ms; 0.35 m camera shake for 200 ms.
- Rib pulse: every breach sends a ring of light down the tube ribs at 600 m/s to the vanishing point, a cheap effect that sells depth and scale on every hit.
- Gear shift: speed springs up over 0.35 s; because hazards are time-locked, the tunnel ahead visibly telescopes away and rushes back in (a free dolly-zoom); the structure palette steps one notch bluer; base FOV +6 deg; ribbons lengthen; star streaks stretch and bunch toward centre; snare roll, riser and a rising arpeggio; a big GEAR stamp with RGB split.
- Flip: 180-degree world roll about a vanishing point that never moves, lingering helix trail, radial blur, landing squash 0.7 > 1.1 > 1.0 in 160 ms, spark ring up the landing lane, 0.2 m camera dip, thud.
- Graze, thread, grind: a side-fan of 20 sparks from the grazed side, a Doppler 'zzip' panned to that side, the runner's rim flashing white; a grind is a continuous spark fountain with a rising saw whine.
- Fly-bys: every dodged hazard whooshes past the camera with a pitch-dropping noise sweep panned by lane, so the player gets constant near-miss feedback even when playing safe.
- Crash: 110 ms hit-stop, camera thrown back 2 m on a spring, horizontal scanline-tear glitch for 300 ms, 40% desaturation for 0.5 s, magenta vignette, the runner flickering as a ghost for 1.2 s, the music low-passed to 400 Hz with every oscillator detuned 3 semitones down for 300 ms (tape-stop), and the nova glow lunging inward.
- Nova proximity, continuous: edge glow, heat-haze distortion at the borders, embers overtaking you toward the vanishing point, the runner casting a long forward shadow up its lane, Geiger-tick hats and a two-tone klaxon on the beat once LEAD drops under 80 m.
- Beat sync: ribs brighten 40% on every kick, city windows twinkle on 16ths, the tunnel bend sways on a 4-bar LFO, ICE countdown rings collapse with pips; the whole world is a metronome.
- Speed dressing: FOV 68 > 92 deg, barrel distortion and radial blur scaled by gear, near-camera speed motes, and ribs stretched along z near the lens so they streak instead of strobing.
- Pickups and judgements: each spark plays the next note of the current arpeggio, orbs ring a rising bell scale, and PERFECT / THREAD / CLEAN text is spawned in the world and flies past the lens with the debris.
- Death: 0.4 s visual slow-down while white-gold wipes in from the edges, the runner dissolves into particles pulled forward, a single sub boom, the distance stamped huge.
