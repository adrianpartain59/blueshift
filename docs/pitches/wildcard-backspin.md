# BACKSPIN  (pitch:wildcard)

**Hook:** It is a runner where you hold one key to un-explode a dead space city in a bubble around you, sprint across road that no longer exists, then let go and drop the explosion on everything inside.

**Fantasy:** You are a chrono-courier sprinting the shattered orbital highway of Halcyon Ring, nine seconds after the city was destroyed. Your stolen backspin drive drags a bubble of 'nine seconds ago' around you for as long as you can hold it: the road, the neon, the rush-hour traffic and the security grid all come back to life inside it. Let go and the catastrophe happens again, right on top of whatever the bubble caught.

**Perspective:** Behind-the-runner chase camera on a 3-lane highway floating in orbit. Pseudo-3D: every vertex gets an OutRun-style curve/hill offset by depth, is pinhole-projected on the CPU, and goes to the 2D batch renderer far-to-near. Camera 3 m above and 7 m behind the runner, horizon at 40% of screen height, runner in the lower third, base FOV 70 degrees. Lanes are 4 m wide; the track is a grid of steps (one step = one 8th note = 12 m) with 24 steps (3 bars) visible before violet fog. All gameplay lives in straight lane/step space; curves, banks and crests are visual only. Primitives: a road cell is one quad with neon edge lines; a missing cell is the same quad pre-cut into 5 triangle shards with a hashed exploded transform; the bubble is a ground ellipse, six dome arcs and a refraction ring in the post shader; the runner is a 14-quad articulated silhouette (visor slit, torso wedge, two-segment limbs) with a ribbon trail; haulers are a rear trapezoid, a top quad and thruster glow sprites; sentries are a diamond with an orbiting ring; lasers and beams are thick additive lines; towers are extruded rectangles with window dot grids; the planet and its broken ring are gradient discs and arcs; billboards are quads of glyph noise from the font atlas.

**Core loop:** Second to second (one decision every 0.25 to 0.5 s): read the floor ahead, which shows two timelines at once (solid hot shapes are real NOW, dashed magenta outlines are real only THEN); pick a lane; inhale (hold) when the present is broken so the past carries you; exhale (release) before the past's own dangers reach you, ideally on the beat and with enemies inside the bubble, so the release becomes a kill and a wall-smashing surge. A typical bar is hold 2 beats, drop, weave 2 beats. The hold is the build-up, the release is the drop, and the level is written so they land on the music. Run to run: 16-bar sectors (25 to 31 s each) step the tempo up; a run ends on the first death, stamps score / best / sector / biggest drop, and rewinds to the start in 1.25 s. The pull to restart is concrete because every death is your own readable mistake: 'I overloaded chasing a 4-kill drop' or 'I let go one step early over the void'.

## Controls
- **Left / Right arrows or A / D (touch: tap the left or right third of the screen)** → Step one lane on a 3-lane track. No auto-repeat, 120 ms input buffer, 60 ms forgiveness against a lethal cell. Fully available while holding backspin.  
  _Feedback:_ 70 ms ease-out slide with an 8 degree body bank and a 2 degree camera roll, a three-frame cyan afterimage, the lane line under your feet flares, and a 20 ms click pitched low/mid/high by lane and panned to that side. Pushing into the track edge gives a dull thud and a 3 px shake instead of silence.
- **HOLD Space (also Up / W / Shift; touch: hold a second thumb anywhere)** → BACKSPIN: inflate the then-bubble. You and everything inside it are in the past for as long as you hold and have tape (max 4 beats).  
  _Feedback:_ A dome of cyan light snaps out from the runner with elastic overshoot, a refraction ring rolls across the screen, floating shards whip inward and lock into road under your stride, dead billboards and guard-rail lights inside the dome flick on, and dashed ghost traffic inside it turns solid. Audio: a vinyl-backspin zip, the whole soundtrack sinks under a resonant low-pass (to 600 Hz in 90 ms) while a lush pad and bell arpeggio fade up, and a riser climbs in pitch for as long as you hold. The dome visibly grows 1.5 steps per beat; at 0.75 beat the rim flashes white with a bell ping (armed).
- **RELEASE Space** → THE DROP: the bubble bursts. Everything from the past inside it is destroyed; if armed, you surge and smash through present-day wreckage for 1 beat.  
  _Feedback:_ One white frame, 50 to 120 ms hitstop (scales with kills), a shock ring tears outward along the highway visibly re-shattering each road cell, FOV kicks 70 -> 84 degrees for 250 ms, the runner lunges with a smear trail, screen shake and a chromatic aberration spike scale with hold time. Audio: sub drop (110 -> 38 Hz), noise crash, the low-pass slams open so the full beat hits, and each kill plays the next note of the current chord as a rising arpeggio. An on-beat release adds a supersaw stab and a PERFECT stamp.
- **Any key on the title or after death (not a gameplay input)** → Start, or instant restart.  
  _Feedback:_ Title: the neon logo detonates under your keypress (the first drop of the game is yours) and you are running in 1.0 s. After death: the whole run scrubs backward along the track in 0.8 s with VHS tearing and a reverse sweep, and the runner re-forms from its own shards on a kick.

## Mechanics
### Backspin (the then-bubble)
- Rules: Every lane-cell of the track has two states: NOW (the ruin) and THEN (nine seconds ago). While the hold key is down, you and everything inside your bubble are in THEN; everything outside stays NOW. The bubble spans all 3 lanes, reaches 1.5 steps behind you and 2.5 steps ahead on press, and its forward reach grows 1.5 steps per beat held, up to 8.5 steps. One step = one 8th note. The state flips on the input frame (the 90 ms shard animation is cosmetic); death is decided only by the cell you occupy and the era you are in. THEN road is always whole, so voids, wrecks and bulkheads cannot hurt you while holding; THEN gates, haulers, sentries and beams can. Other-era objects are always visible as dashed outlines and have no collision. Engaging needs at least 0.5 beat of tape; 80 ms of late-input forgiveness applies on every lethal edge.
- Why fun: It is a held breath. The input has duration, so the tension is physically in your finger, and the response is the largest one possible: the world itself rebuilds under your feet. Running on road that only exists because you are holding a key never stops feeling like getting away with something.
- Interplay: The bubble's size is the charge meter for the Drop, holding it spends Tape, and it doubles the lane problem: every row asks which lane AND which era. Lane steps stay fully available while holding, so the hardest passages are holding across a void while weaving through past traffic that your own bubble just made real.
### The Drop (burst + snap)
- Rules: Releasing the key bursts the bubble on that frame. BURST: every THEN entity inside the bubble (sentries, haulers) is destroyed, scoring 100 x chain x number destroyed in that burst, and each kill refunds 0.5 beat of tape; kills are permanent. SNAP: if the hold lasted at least 0.75 beat (rim white = armed), you get BREACH for 1 beat (2 steps): any NOW wreck cell you enter is smashed for 250 x chain instead of killing you. PERFECT: a release within +/-50 ms of a quarter-note doubles that drop's score and refunds 0.25 beat. Gates, voids and bulkheads are never destroyed. The runner's true speed never changes (position is locked to the beat clock); the surge is FOV, lunge and speed lines.
- Why fun: Letting go is the attack. The release has a bang proportional to how long you dared to hold, it lands on the music, and it turns a defensive state into a wind-up: you are not hiding in the past, you are loading it.
- Interplay: It needs Backspin to charge, and its targets only become real inside the bubble, so to kill something you must first make it able to kill you. It refunds Tape, feeds Chain, and gives lanes a second purpose (line up outside a sentry's beam, then drop). Armed breach makes release timing matter against present-day walls, not just past enemies.
### Tape and Chain
- Rules: Tape is 4 beats of hold. It drains 1 beat per beat held and refills 1 beat per beat released, plus 0.5 per kill and 0.25 per PERFECT. If it hits zero while you are holding: OVERLOAD. The bubble implodes with no burst and no snap, backspin is locked out for 2 beats, and chain is halved; over a void, you fall. Chain is the score multiplier, x1 to x16: +1 for every drop that kills, breaches, or is PERFECT after an armed hold. An 8-beat fuse runs under it; when it burns out, chain drops by 1 and the fuse restarts. Tape is shown as a ring at the runner's feet and a thin bar top-centre; under 1 beat it strobes amber with an accelerating two-note alarm.
- Why fun: It makes every hold a bet. A 50% duty cycle is free forever, so rhythmic play is sustainable, but anything greedier has to be paid for with kills and clean timing, and the bill arrives two bars later as a void you can no longer cross.
- Interplay: Tape caps Backspin, the Drop refunds it, and Chain rewards exactly the releases that are riskiest to set up: big bubbles full of live enemies, on the beat. Authored void lengths (up to 7 steps = 3.5 beats) are tuned against the 4-beat capacity so greed and survival draw on the same pool.

## First 30 seconds
- 0-2s: Any key on the title. The intact neon logo detonates under your keypress (your first drop), sub boom, the camera falls through the shards and lands behind the runner already at full sprint on a floating, broken highway. Kick and bass only. No text.
- 2-5s: Establishing run. Burnt orange wreckage and dead towers drift past; a wreck slab passes harmlessly in the left lane to show what solid-and-hot looks like; faint dashed outlines of traffic stream down the road like ghosts. A wreck sits dead ahead in the centre lane with two chevrons painted on the floor: sidestep. Spark graze, pitched click. (First run only: the world eases to a standstill on a filtered drone until a lane key is pressed.)
- 5-7s: The road simply ends. Six steps of open space, shards of the missing highway hanging in a cloud, and one pulsing key-cap glyph with a rewind symbol hovering over the edge. (First run only: the world holds at the lip until the key goes down.)
- 7-9s: Hold. The dome snaps out, the soundtrack sinks under a low-pass and a pad blooms, shards whip into place under every stride, guard-rail lights chase ahead of you and a dead billboard overhead flickers alive. You are running across a bridge that stopped existing nine seconds ago. The riser climbs, the rim flashes white.
- 9-10s: The glyph flips to an open-hand release icon on a floor marker placed on the downbeat past the far lip. Let go: white frame, a shock ring races up the highway re-shattering it, and the full drums enter on that exact beat because your release is what triggers them. PERFECT stamps the screen. This is the whoa.
- 10-15s: PUMP. Three 2-step voids, each landing on the beat: hold-drop, hold-drop, hold-drop. The world inhales and explodes in time with the kick; chain ticks x2, x3, x4 and the chain counter appears on the HUD for the first time.
- 15-19s: A dashed magenta hauler fills your lane and passes straight through your body (scanline ripple, doppler hum): ghosts are harmless in the present. Then a void with a dashed magenta laser gate two steps past it: hold across, watch the bubble's rim turn the gate solid ahead of you, and let go before it.
- 19-24s: FIRING LINE. Three dashed sentries hover on solid road just past a short void. You hold across the void, they turn real inside the dome, you release: pop-pop-pop in a rising arpeggio, three bursts of hot triangles, the tape ring visibly refills, TRIPLE flies to the score.
- 24-28s: BATTERING RAM. A long void, two steps of road, then a full-width wall of wreckage with a magenta gate ghosted on the same step. Three countdown chevrons are painted on the road on the beat. Hold (armed), release on the last chevron: 100 ms freeze, the wall becomes 200 white-hot triangles parting around the camera, FOV slams out.
- 28-31s: You burst out of the debris into open space: the cracked planet, the broken ring and a mile-high neon tower fill the sky, the lead melody enters, a gate ring swallows you with a snare roll, the tempo steps up to 132 and the word RUSH flashes once.

## Signature moment
The bridge that is not there. The runner sprints off the end of a shattered highway into open space; a dome of cyan light snaps out and the road rebuilds itself shard by shard half a stride ahead of each footfall, dead neon signs flickering alive inside the dome while everything outside stays a burnt orange graveyard; dashed ghost haulers turn solid as the dome's rim touches them. Then, on the downbeat, the dome bursts: a shock ring tears up the highway, three sentries pop in a rising arpeggio, and the runner punches through a wall of wreckage in a spray of white-hot triangles as the bass drops. The still frame: one screen split by the dome's rim into a living city and a dead one, with the runner mid-stride on glass.

## Hazards and patterns
Vocabulary (6 hazards, 1 pickup). NOW, drawn solid in orange/red: VOID (road cells missing; you fall), WRECK (1-step slab in a lane; lethal, but smashed if you are breaching), BULKHEAD (fused full-width wall; cannot be breached; in the past it is an open, lit doorway). THEN, drawn in magenta, dashed outside the bubble and solid inside it: GATE (laser curtain over 1 to 3 lanes of one step; indestructible, simply absent in the present), HAULER (cargo drone 2 steps long, parked or drifting forward at half your speed; dies in a burst), SENTRY (1 cell plus a beam covering the 2 steps in front of it in its lane; dies in a burst). MEMORY (cyan diamond, exists only in the past, 50 x chain). Late twist, PARADOX PAIR: a hauler joined by a thin white dotted thread to its own future wreck 4 to 6 steps down the same lane; burst the hauler and the wreck never happened. Patterns (1 to 2 bars, hand-authored as two 3-lane rows per step, one for each era): 1) LEAP OF FAITH: full-width void 4 to 6 steps, past empty; hold and run. 2) PUMP: void 2 steps, then road 2 steps carrying a full-width gate, repeated 4 times; hold and release on alternating beats, tape-neutral, every release can be PERFECT. 3) FIRING LINE: 2-step void, then three sentries abreast on solid road; keep holding one step past the lip to pop all three, or bail early for nothing. 4) BATTERING RAM: void, 2 steps of road, then a full-width wreck wall with a full-width gate on the same step; the only answer is an armed release in the last 2 steps so you breach it. 5) RUSH HOUR: 6-step full-width void with haulers staggered left, right, centre in the past; hold the whole way and weave, then release at the lip to burst whatever is still inside the dome. 6) ZIPPER: 8 steps alternating 1-step void and 1-step full gate; an 8th-note trill on the hold key. 7) FORK: left lane is a slalom of wrecks on solid road (free, graze points), right lane is a 5-step void strip lined with memories and guarded by a sentry (costs 2.5 beats of tape, pays 5 memories and a kill), centre is walled; pick your greed. 8) BULKHEAD RUN: a bulkhead forces you into the past, then 4 steps of corridor with gates covering two lanes at a time on alternating sides, then a sentry waiting outside for your exit drop. 9) PARADOX: a lone hauler threaded to a 3-lane wreck wall 5 steps on; burst the hauler early and the wall dissolves into upward sparks, or ignore the thread and breach it the hard way. 10) STARVE: two pumps straight into a 7-step void; you only have the tape if your earlier releases were clean.

## Readability
Five hard rules. (1) Hue is lethality: only hot hues kill (orange-red in the present, magenta in the past); everything safe or helpful is cool (cyan, blue-violet, white). (2) Line style is timeline: solid, filled and blooming means it can kill you right now; a thin dashed outline at 35% brightness with no fill and no bloom means it belongs to the other timeline and you can run straight through it. The bubble's rim is the literal boundary where dashed becomes solid, so the player never has to remember a hidden mode. (3) The floor is the chart: every lethal cell paints its lane tile (solid orange for a wreck, a missing tile with a glowing orange lip for a void, magenta dashed or solid for past hazards), so the whole problem reads as flat coloured tiles on three lanes, like a note highway, without parsing 3D shapes. Road that exists only in the past is drawn as translucent cyan glass with an orange dashed border: glass under your feet means do not let go. (4) The lane corridor is sacred: nothing decorative ever enters the track volume up to 2 runner-heights. Scenery lives beyond the guard rails, is capped at 45% luminance, uses only vertical and horizontal lines, gets depth fog and never pulses. Hazards are the only things with diagonal chevrons, a black keyline and a 6% scale pulse on the beat. (5) Effects never hide the answer: chromatic aberration, distortion and grain are weighted to the screen edges and spikes are capped at 120 ms, the centre column stays sharp, the shard-assembly animation finishes in 90 ms and is always resolved at least 1 step ahead of the runner, and particles are never hot-hued unless they came from something that just died. In sectors 1 and 2 a row is either an era question (full-width) or a lane question (one timeline), never both.

## Risk / reward
Score: 10 per step x chain; memory 50 x chain; burst kills 100 x chain x kills-in-that-burst (a triple pays 9x a single); breached wreck 250 x chain; graze 25 x chain; PERFECT doubles that drop. Chain runs x1 to x16: +1 on every drop that kills, breaches, or is PERFECT after an armed hold; an 8-beat fuse drops it by 1 if you stop making live drops; OVERLOAD halves it. Greed loops, all built from the one verb: (a) Hold longer to grow the bubble and catch more enemies in a single burst, while tape drains toward overload and the past's traffic and beams close in. (b) Wait for the beat to earn PERFECT while a sentry beam is one step away. (c) Memories exist only in the past and are laid over voids and beside haulers, so collecting them means staying in THEN longer than survival requires. (d) Kills refund 0.5 beat of tape each, so aggression literally buys more time in the past, while cautious play slowly starves. (e) Forks offer a free present-day lane against a paid, lucrative past lane. (f) Tape spent on greed now may be the tape you need two bars later for a 7-step void.

## Escalation
Tempo is the speedometer; the track is locked to the beat, so speed, music and difficulty rise together. Sector 1 WAKE (0:00): 124 BPM, 4.1 steps/s (about 50 m/s), hazards only on quarter notes, voids up to 6 steps. Sector 2 RUSH (0:31): 132 BPM, 8th-note placement, parked haulers, forks, first memories. Sector 3 CASCADE (1:00): 140 BPM, both timelines overlap (haulers over voids), sentry beams, drifting convoys, the road starts to bank and crest. Sector 4 FRACTURE (1:27): 148 BPM, zippers, bulkhead runs, paradox pairs, 7-step voids, breathers shrink from 1 bar to half a bar. Sector 5 SINGULARITY (1:53): 156 BPM, 5.2 steps/s (about 62 m/s), everything mixed, sunrise set piece. Sector 6+ OVERTIME (2:18 onward): tempo holds at 156, hard-pattern weight rises 10% per sector and tape capacity shrinks 0.25 beat per sector down to 3 beats. Every sector is 16 bars and ends in a 2-bar gate-ring fly-through with a snare roll and the tempo step. Expected first-timer death: sector 2 to 3 (0:45 to 1:20). A strong run reaches sector 8 to 9 (3:30 to 4:00).

## Audio direction
Synthwave / darksynth in F minor, 4/4, 124 BPM rising 8 BPM per sector to 156. Progression Fm - Db - Ab - Eb, one bar each (B phrase: Fm - Cm - Db - Eb). All synthesized: kick (sine with pitch envelope 150 -> 45 Hz), gated snare (noise + 180 Hz body), closed and open hats (high-passed noise), 16th-note saw bass through a resonant low-pass over a sub sine, pulse-wave arpeggio, detuned-saw pad, saw lead with vibrato and dotted-8th delay, generated-noise convolution reverb, sidechain duck from the kick. Sector layering: S1 kick + bass + hats (the full drums are brought in by the player's first drop); S2 adds arpeggio; S3 adds the lead melody; S4 adds a counter-melody and 16th hats; S5 adds supersaw chord stabs and octave bass. Chain layering: at x4 the arpeggio doubles an octave up, at x8 the lead gains a harmony. Two mixes of the same sequence run at once: the NOW mix (dry, driving, full drums) and the THEN mix (half-time kick, lush pad, glassy bell arpeggio). Holding sweeps a low-pass over the NOW mix (18 kHz -> 600 Hz, Q 8, 90 ms) and fades the THEN mix up; releasing slams it back, so every hold is a filter build and every release is a drop. Key SFX, tuned to the current chord: backspin zip (reverse noise sweep 4 kHz -> 500 Hz plus a vinyl pitch-down of a fifth); hold riser (saw + noise, band-pass 300 Hz -> 3 kHz across 4 beats, tremolo accelerating from 16ths to 32nds); armed ping (bell, root + 12); drop (sub 110 -> 38 Hz over 350 ms, noise crash 600 ms); kill plucks climbing chord tones on 32nds; breach crunch (noise through a comb at 180 Hz plus a tom drop 200 -> 60 Hz); lane clicks at three pitches; graze whip; memory bells walking up a pentatonic scale; low-tape alarm (two notes a minor second apart, doubling in rate); overload tape-chew; death tape-stop (all music voices glide down two octaves in 400 ms); restart reverse sweep ending on a kick.

## Failure and restart
You die by occupying a lethal cell of the era you are currently in: falling into a void or hitting a wreck or bulkhead in the present (without breach), or touching a gate, hauler, sentry or beam in the past. Overload over a void is the classic greedy death. 80 ms of late-input forgiveness is applied before death is confirmed. Death beat: 90 ms freeze, the runner shatters, the music tape-stops, a three-line stamp appears immediately (SCORE, BEST, SECTOR + biggest drop) and the cause of death stays spotlit. Input is accepted and buffered from 250 ms after death. Restart is the game's own verb: the entire run backspins to step 0 in 0.8 s (camera scrubbing backward along the track, VHS tear, reverse sweep), the runner re-forms from its shards and lands on a downbeat. Minimum death-to-control: 0.25 + 0.8 + up to 0.2 s of beat sync = 1.25 s. Title-to-run: 1.0 s. First-run-only assists (the world eases to a standstill on a filtered drone until the prompted key is pressed, for the first sidestep, first hold, first release and first gate) switch off permanently once used, and every later run uses a 6-bar veteran opening so you are in real patterns by 6 s. Each run reseeds the order of patterns within a sector's pool; pattern internals stay hand-designed.

## Bot strategy
The level is a deterministic grid: per step and lane, one NOW cell type and one THEN cell type, plus entities whose cells at the moment the runner arrives are known in advance because runner position is locked to the beat clock (drifting haulers are rasterized into the grid by arrival time). Bot state = (16th-note tick, lane, holding, tape, continuous hold time, breach ticks left, lockout ticks left, set of killed entity ids in view). Actions per tick = lane -1 / 0 / +1 x hold on / off. It runs a beam search 24 steps (3 bars) ahead, pruning any state that enters a lethal cell or overloads over a void. Survival mode (automated tests): maximise slack, preferring plans in which every input can be 2 ticks early or late and still live, tie-breaking toward NOW and the centre lane; it asserts that every generated segment is survivable from every legal entry state (any lane, tape at or above the pattern's declared minimum). Showboat mode (attract screen): maximise score, snapping releases to quarter notes for PERFECT and delaying drops until the bubble holds the most entities. The bot injects synthetic key events into the same input layer at fixed 120 Hz sim ticks, so it exercises exactly the code a human does, and a seed plus an input log replays bit-identically.

## Tech notes
Timing: the simulation is a fixed 120 Hz step slaved to the audio clock through a tempo map; runner step position is a pure function of song time, so sync cannot drift and nothing in gameplay changes true speed. Hitstop and micro slow-mo are render-time only: the render clock stalls, then catches up over 200 ms, while the sim never leaves the audio clock. The only true transport pause is the first-run tutorial hold. Collision: no physics; one cell lookup per tick for (lane, step, era) plus a deferred-death timer for the 80 ms forgiveness. Level: patterns authored as ASCII, two 3-character rows per step (NOW and THEN), 1 to 2 bars each, tagged with sector range, tape cost and entry requirements; the generator concatenates them and the bot verifies the result before it scrolls into view. Rendering: one dynamic vertex buffer of coloured, optionally textured triangles; the CPU does bend + pinhole projection per vertex; draw far-to-near by step (already sorted), alpha fills first, then additive glows; roughly 6 to 10 thousand quads per frame. The whole un-explosion effect is one scalar per object: a smoothstep over a 1.5-step band of (bubble reach minus distance to the runner) that lerps each shard between a hashed exploded transform and its assembled transform; the same code runs the title shatter, wreck breach, death and restart. The bubble is a ground ellipse, six arcs and one ring uniform. Post chain: scene to a half-float target, bright-pass, 5-level half-res bloom, then one composite shader with edge-weighted chromatic aberration, up to 4 shock-ring distortions (centre, radius, strength uniforms), an era colour grade inside the projected bubble mask, scanlines, vignette, grain, and a VHS tear parameter for the rewind. Audio: a 25 ms lookahead scheduler with a 120 ms horizon; music bus -> era low-pass -> sidechain gain -> compressor; the SFX bus bypasses the era filter so feedback is never muffled; action SFX fire on the input frame and only musical decorations quantize. Cheap: the two-era logic, the bubble, the bot, the shards. Expensive: bloom fill-rate (keep it half-res), particle count (cap 3,000), and above all tuning ghost-versus-solid contrast, which is where polish time should go.

## Biggest risk
Two timelines drawn on one road can turn into visual soup, and if that happens the verb collapses into guesswork, or the player ignores the ghosts and it plays like a hold-to-jump. Mitigations, in order of importance: the bubble makes the rule spatial instead of modal (inside the rim the past is real, outside it is a dashed ghost), so there is never a hidden state to remember; the floor-tile chart reduces every row to three coloured tiles; in sectors 1 and 2 a row is either a full-width era question or a single-timeline lane question, never both; a scripted ghost hauler passes harmlessly through the player at 16 s to prove ghosts are safe; vocabulary is capped at 3 present and 3 past hazard types; every pattern is bot-verified to leave at least 2 ticks of slack per input in the sector where it is introduced; first-run prompts guarantee the core lessons land without a death. The hold-to-jump reading is killed by design at 20 s and 26 s, when the same release first wipes a firing line and then punches through a wall: from then on the hold is a wind-up, not a hop.

## Why this wins
One key, and the entire world is the feedback: no other runner verb gets this much visible and audible response from a single finger, and it costs the engine almost nothing (one scalar per shard, one filter sweep). It is a held breath, so tension and release are built into the input itself and map one-to-one onto the soundtrack's build and drop; the player performs the music by surviving. It teaches itself in one obstacle (the road is gone, hold, the road is back) and then keeps revealing consequences for minutes: the past has its own killers, letting go is your only weapon, bigger holds mean bigger drops, kills buy more past. Even death and restart are the same verb, so the whole game has one identity. It is strange enough to describe to a friend in one sentence, yet underneath it is a 3-lane, 2-layer grid that a bot can solve, a generator can verify, and a one-shot build can polish to a very high level. And the first screenshot, a dome of living cyan city carried through an orange graveyard, is unmistakably this game.

- While holding: riser pitch and a 16th-note rim tick accelerate; the rim brightens from cyan to white at the armed point (0.75 beat) with a bell ping; under 1 beat of tape the rim strobes amber and a two-note alarm doubles in rate.
