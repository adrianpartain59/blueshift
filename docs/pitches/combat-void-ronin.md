# VOID RONIN  (pitch:combat)

**Hook:** You never dodge the enemies: they are the only road across the void, and every kill refuels the blink that carries you to the next one.

**Fantasy:** You are a ronin courier sprinting the light-rails of a derelict neon megacity ring in orbit around a gas giant, plasma katana in hand and a blink-drive on your back. Security drones swarm the rails and the rails keep ending. You blink through drone after drone, each kill recharging the next blink, drawing constellations of cyan light across open space.

**Perspective:** 2D side view, orthographic, auto-scrolling left to right. Three parallel horizontal light-rails (TOP, MID, LOW) 2.5 cells apart; 1 cell is the world unit, about 24 cells visible across, player held at 28% from the left (about 17 cells of look-ahead). Side view is chosen because horizontal and diagonal slashes, trails and bisected enemies read perfectly and are exactly what line/quad/glow primitives do best. Camera is a critically damped spring (8 Hz): on a strike the runner visibly lunges ahead on screen and the camera catches up in about 0.25s; zoom eases from 1.0 to 0.86 as speed rises so look-ahead never drops below 1.1s; roll up to 1 degree, kill zoom-punch +4%. Drawing: runner is a forward-leaning triangle torso, two-bone sine-driven quad legs, a katana line and a 12-segment verlet scarf ribbon whose length is the shield count. Rails are glowing lines with dashes streaming backward. Six parallax layers: star points, a ringed gas giant (gradient disc, ring ellipses, scanline bands), far megacity towers pointing both up and down (quads with window dots), mid girders and holo-billboards (font-atlas glyph noise), the play band, and fast dark foreground struts that only pass above and below the play band.

**Core loop:** Second to second: read which amber target the lock reticle is on, snap rails to change it or to avoid red, press Strike to blink through the target, then use the 0.5s hang to pick the next one. On rails you position; over voids you chain. Ten-second loop: every 5 kills in a chain forges a shield layer and adds a music layer; a mistake burns one. Run to run: reach OVERDRIVE (everything red turns gold and cuttable), survive deeper phases, beat best score and max chain. Procedural assembly of hand-made patterns keeps each run fresh.

## Controls
- **Up / Down (W/S or arrows; touch: swipe on left half)** → Rail Snap: blink to the adjacent rail in 60ms. No cooldown, 100ms input buffer, also works mid-hang over a void. Changes which targets are in your lock cone.  
  _Feedback:_ A vertical cyan lightning ribbon joins the two rails, 3 fading afterimages, the scarf whips. The rail you land on flares white and wobbles like a plucked string for 0.3s. Sound: a 50ms saw zip, pitched up for Up (880 to 1320 Hz) and down for Down (880 to 587 Hz), plus a soft sub thump. Camera nudges 6px in the snap direction.
- **Space / J / X (touch: tap right half)** → Strike: if a target is locked, blink-slash through it in 90ms and come out 0.6 cells behind it on its rail (free, repeatable). If nothing is locked, a plain 4-cell forward dash in 110ms that costs 1 of 2 dash cells and has 0.25s recovery.  
  _Feedback:_ The runner becomes a white-hot line with a crescent slash arc. On a kill: 50ms hitstop, the enemy splits in two along the exact slash angle and the halves spin apart, a shockwave ring distorts the screen, a chromatic burst, the camera lunges, the trail segment stays lit for 1.2s. Sound: a bandpassed noise shing sweeping 2k to 8k Hz plus an FM bell on the next note of the D minor pentatonic, rising with the chain. A whiff gives only a dull low whoosh and a dimmer trail, so the difference is felt instantly.
- **Any key (title and results screens)** → Start or restart the run.  
  _Feedback:_ A 0.35s rewind-glitch wipe with a reverse cymbal, then the kick drum lands exactly as control is handed over.

## Mechanics
### Homing Strike with refund and hang (enemies are the road)
- Rules: Lock cone: the nearest amber target 1 to 7 cells ahead on your rail or an adjacent rail, with a clear straight line (no red on the segment). A reticle and a thin amber line always show the current lock. Strike travels in 90ms, is immune to amber contact during travel plus 100ms after, and ends 0.6 cells past the target on the target's rail. A kill costs nothing and grants a 0.5s hang: gravity off, drifting forward at run speed, a shrinking ring shows the timer. Strike recovery after a kill is 0.08s. With no lock, Strike is a plain 4-cell dash costing 1 of 2 dash cells (regen 1 per 1.5s while on a rail) with 0.25s recovery; it crosses gaps but not red. Off a rail and not hanging: 0.2s coyote time, then you fall, which counts as a hit.
- Why fun: One button always does something spectacular, never whiffs when the reticle is on, and turns the obstacles into fuel and footing. Chains over a void are a rhythm of commit, kill, breathe, commit.
- Interplay: Rail Snap decides what the cone can see and whether the line is clear. Each kill feeds the Overclock chain. Voids make the refund matter: plain dashes cover at most 8 cells, so longer gaps can only be crossed on kills.
### Rail Snap (positioning as aiming)
- Rules: Three rails. Snap takes 60ms, has no cooldown, and is allowed during hang. On TOP the cone sees TOP and MID; on LOW it sees LOW and MID; on MID it sees all three. Red hardlight blocks rails and blocks strike lines. Shielders carry a red front plate: they cannot be locked from their own rail, only from an adjacent rail (a diagonal flank). Body contact with any amber enemy without striking is a hit.
- Why fun: A safe, instant, expressive move that is both the dodge and the aim. Snapping mid-hang to re-route a chain around a mine feels like a decision made at the speed of thought.
- Interplay: It is the only way to change the strike target, to flank shielders, to leave a lancer's firing rail, and to find a landing rail when a hang is about to expire.
### Overclock: chain forges shields, shields ignite OVERDRIVE
- Rules: Chain: +1 per kill, kept alive by killing again within 3.0s. Every 5th kill of a chain forges 1 shield layer (start with 1, max 3), shown as scarf length and as music layers. Any hit (red contact, enemy contact, falling) burns 1 layer, resets the chain, gives 1.0s of invulnerability and recalls you to the next rail if you fell. A hit at 0 layers is death. At 3 layers the next 5-chain triggers OVERDRIVE for 6s: every red object turns gold and becomes a lockable, cuttable target, run speed +20%, hang 0.7s, contact immunity. In the last 0.5s gold flickers; on expiry any former-red within 3 cells of you is destroyed so the hand-back is never unfair.
- Why fun: Aggression is literally your armor, your soundtrack and your power-up. Playing safe leaves you fragile and the music thin; playing hard makes you tanky and loud, then makes the whole level edible.
- Interplay: It is fed only by Strike kills, protected by Snap, and it redefines the color rule for 6 seconds, turning every wall you had to route around into something to go through.

## First 30 seconds
- 0-2s: Title screen is the attract-mode bot playing behind the logo. Any key: the runner blinks through the amber logo letters, slicing them in half; they fall away, kick and sub bass start, the runner is already sprinting on the MID rail with the gas giant filling the background.
- 2-5s: One fat amber drone ahead on MID with a large rotating reticle and a pulsing Space key glyph drawn in the world. As it enters range time eases down to 5% speed and the music low-passes until Strike is pressed. Press: white line, 50ms hitstop, drone bisected, shockwave, bell note, bass filter opens. The player has learned the game without reading.
- 5-9s: The MID rail ends at a flashing end-cap. Three harmless amber nodes float over a 12-cell void, 4 cells apart. Strike, strike, strike: three rising notes, three trail segments left glowing, landing sparks on the far rail. The player has learned that enemies are the road.
- 9-13s: A red striped hardlight wall on MID with Up and Down arrow glyphs; one more slow-motion beat the first time only. Snap: lightning ribbon, rail pluck. Immediately a drone on the new rail, kill 5 of the chain: a shield ring forges around the runner, the scarf grows a segment, hi-hats and the open bassline enter on the next beat.
- 13-19s: First mixed pattern at full speed: a wall on TOP, drones staggered MID, LOW, MID so the auto-lock zigzags the runner diagonally past the wall. No prompts. The multiplier pops to x2 with a number punch.
- 19-24s: A lancer at the far end of LOW draws a thin dashed red telegraph line down its rail with a rising whine. Two drones lead toward it; killing it before the beam fires is the natural result of chaining. Chain 10: second shield, snare and arpeggio join.
- 24-30s: The Leap. All three rails end, the camera zooms out to show the whole ringed planet, and seven amber drones hang in a W shape across a 28-cell void. Seven strikes in about 2.5 seconds, each hit-stopped, the cyan zigzag left burning like a drawn constellation. The landing on the far rail hits exactly on a downbeat: the pad and full beat drop, the title VOID RONIN slams across the background and shatters as the run continues.

## Signature moment
The Leap into OVERDRIVE: the rails end, the camera pulls back to reveal the ringed gas giant, and the runner crosses open space by blinking through a W-shaped line of drones, each kill a freeze-framed slash, leaving a glowing cyan constellation connecting seven explosions. On the last kill the music cuts to one beat of silence, then every red wall on screen flips to gold, the track jumps up a whole tone, and the runner cuts straight through a wall that was untouchable five seconds earlier.

## Hazards and patterns
AMBER (cut it): NODE, a harmless diamond placed only over voids, pure stepping stone. DRONE, static on a rail, contact hurts. SHIELDER, red front plate, lockable only from an adjacent rail. WEAVER, oscillates between two rails on a 1.0s period. LANCER, 0.6s dashed telegraph then a 0.4s red beam down its whole rail; kill it first or leave the rail. FREIGHTER (set piece), spans all rails with 3 amber cores that open in sequence; each core kill rebounds you into a hang.
RED (never touch): HARDLIGHT WALL blocking 1 or 2 rails for 1 to 3 cells. MINE, a red diamond that blocks strike lines. LASER GATE, a vertical beam across all rails that is on for 2 beats and off for 2 beats. SWEEPER, a beam that steps rail to rail once per beat with the next rail telegraphed. VOID, a missing rail segment marked by a flashing end-cap.
Patterns: (1) Stepping Stones: 12-cell void on all rails, 3 nodes on MID at 4-cell spacing. (2) Cassiopeia: 20 to 28 cell void, drones alternating MID, TOP, MID, LOW, MID so the chain draws a W. (3) Flank: shielder on MID with a wall on LOW; you must be on TOP to strike diagonally down through it and land behind. (4) Lancer Race: lancers at the ends of TOP and LOW firing alternately, a ladder of drones leading to each; kill it before the beam or bail. (5) Minefield Thread: a void constellation where the nearest target from MID is line-blocked by a mine; snap mid-hang to re-aim around it. (6) Bait: a drone sitting 2 cells in front of a wall on its own rail; strike it and you must snap within 0.2s, or approach from the other rail and take the next drone instead. (7) Weaver Gate: two weavers in antiphase in front of a wall on MID; strike when the weaver is on a rail with a clear exit. (8) Gate Rush: a laser gate preceded by 3 drones; the chain's forward gain carries you through during the off beats, dawdling meets it closed. (9) Freighter: three core kills with sweeper beams between, then the hull splits along your trail.

## Readability
Color is the verb, with no exceptions. Cyan (#00E5FF) is you, your rails and your trail: safe. Amber (#FFB000) means press Strike. Red-magenta (#FF1744 with a white-hot core) means never touch. The background is restricted to violet, indigo and dark teal at 35% luminance or less and 40% saturation or less; red, amber and pure cyan never appear in decoration, and the bloom threshold is set so only gameplay colors bloom hard. Shape backs up color for color-blind players: red hazards are solid filled blocks with moving diagonal stripes, a 2px black keyline and an 8 Hz flicker; amber targets are hollow rings or diamonds with a rotating bracket; safe elements are thin lines. The lock reticle plus the thin amber line from runner to target shows exactly what Strike will do right now; a blocked lock shows a grey bracket with a red slash. Every future red (lancer beam, sweeper step, gate switching on) is telegraphed 0.6s ahead by a thin dashed red line. A dark gutter gradient dims the background 30% behind the play band, foreground struts never cross it, hazards draw on top of all particles, and particles live under 0.4s. Shake is capped at 6px. Camera zoom-out keeps at least 1.1s of look-ahead at every speed.

## Risk / reward
Score: 10 per cell travelled; 100 per kill times the multiplier, where multiplier is 1 + floor(chain / 5), max x10. Close Cut: striking from 2 cells or less doubles the kill (riskier because contact hurts). Clean: a kill within 70ms of an eighth-note adds 50%. Aerial: each consecutive kill made during a hang adds +25. Overdrive kills count double, and former-red walls and mines score as kills. Greed loops: optional high-road constellations branch off over open void toward gold data cores (500 times multiplier) with no rail beneath, so you bet a shield on finishing the chain; plain dashes buy speed to reach the next target before the 3.0s chain timer expires but spend the cells that save you at a gap; red-walled caches can only be opened in Overdrive, so you route toward them when it is about to trigger; holding a 3-shield state tempts you to chase the next 5-chain through the densest lane to ignite Overdrive.

## Escalation
Phase 1 DOCKS, 0:00 to 0:35, 9 cells/s: nodes, drones, single walls, short voids that always have a rail at another height. Phase 2 SPINE, 0:35 to 1:15, 10.5 cells/s: shielders, lancers, longer constellations, first data-core branches. Set piece at about 1:15: the first FREIGHTER, 10 seconds. Phase 3 CORE, 1:25 to 2:10, 12 cells/s: weavers, minefields, beat-toggled laser gates, bait placements, two-pattern overlaps. Phase 4 SUNRISE, 2:10 to 3:00, 13.5 cells/s: the sun crests the planet's limb and floods the scene, sweepers appear, patterns are chained without rest rails, a second Freighter with escorts. After 3:00 the game loops all pools endlessly at +0.5 cells/s per loop up to 16 cells/s with a new palette each loop. Each phase ends with a 2-bar riser and 3 seconds of empty rail as a breath. New players typically die between 1:00 and 2:00; mastery runs pass 4:00.

## Visual direction
Palette: void black-violet #07040F, deep indigo #140A2E, structure violet #2A1458, dark teal #0E3A46, planet bands #3B1E6E to #6A2C8F with a pale ring #9C8CFF at 30% opacity. Gameplay colors: cyan #00E5FF (player, rails, trail), amber #FFB000 (targets), red-magenta #FF1744 with core #FFE9F0 (hazards), gold #FFD86B (Overdrive), white #FFFFFF for cores of slashes only. Phase accents shift the background only: DOCKS violet, SPINE teal, CORE deep blue with a magenta-free purple haze, SUNRISE warm peach #FFB199 rim light on the planet limb while the play band keeps its strict colors. Layers: twinkling star points; the ringed gas giant with slow band drift; far megacity towers hanging both up and down from the ring; cargo ships crossing slowly; mid girders, antennas and scrolling glyph billboards; the play band; foreground struts. Set pieces: The Leap (camera pull-back over open space), the Freighter (a wall of plating that splits along your cut), the orbital sunrise at 2:10 with a bloom flare sweeping the screen, and the Overdrive palette flip. Post: bloom, mild barrel distortion, scanlines at 12%, vignette, event-driven chromatic aberration.

## Audio direction
128 BPM, D minor, 8-bar loop Dm, Dm, Bb, C, dark synthwave. Kick: sine swept 150 to 45 Hz over 180ms with a click. Snare/clap: bandpassed noise at 1.8 kHz with a 200 Hz triangle body. Hats: high-passed noise at 8 kHz, 30ms closed, 120ms open. Bass: two detuned saws through a resonant low-pass with an envelope, sixteenth-note octave jumps, sidechained to the kick by gain automation. Pad: four detuned saws per note with a slow filter sweep and chorus from short modulated delays. Arp: pulse wave sixteenths with a dotted-eighth feedback delay. Lead: 7-voice supersaw with 40ms portamento and vibrato. Master: soft-clip waveshaper into a compressor. Adaptive layers by shield count, switched on the next beat with a fill: 0 shields is kick plus a muffled bass (400 Hz low-pass) and a heartbeat; 1 adds hats and the open bass; 2 adds snare and arp; 3 adds pad and counter-arp; OVERDRIVE adds the lead and transposes everything up 2 semitones. Sound effects fire instantly and are pitch-quantized, not time-quantized: strike shing (noise through a bandpass sweeping 2k to 8k Hz), kill bell (FM, next pentatonic note of the chain, wrapping after two octaves), whiff (low airy whoosh), snap zip, landing thump, shield forge (rising fifth plus reverse cymbal), hit (square dropping 400 to 60 Hz, noise crunch, low-pass slam), lancer charge (sine rising 300 to 1200 Hz over 0.6s) and beam (noisy saw burst), Overdrive ignition (one beat of silence then impact), death tape-stop with a sub boom, 2-bar noise risers before set pieces.

## Failure and restart
A hit with shields left burns one, resets the chain and gives 1.0s of invulnerability; if you fell you are recalled onto the next rail ahead. A hit at 0 shields is death: 250ms freeze frame, shatter, tape-stop, and the results panel (score, best, max chain, distance) slams in over the still-drifting world. Input is locked for 0.65s to avoid accidental skips; any key then triggers a 0.35s rewind-glitch wipe and you spawn already running with 1 shield and a drone 1.2s ahead, so the first slash happens within 2 seconds. Death to control is about 1.0s. Restarts skip the slow-motion prompts and the logo, and the music restarts on a downbeat with the kick.

## Bot strategy
The world is an ordered list of entities (type, rail, x, parameters) plus rail segments per rail; moving things (weavers, sweepers, gates, lancer timers) are pure functions of simulation time, and the sim is a fixed 120 Hz step with a seeded RNG. The bot calls the same lock function the game uses and applies rules in priority order each tick. (1) If hanging or airborne over a void: strike if a lock exists and its landing spot is safe (a rail there, or another lock reachable within the hang); otherwise snap to a rail that exists under the current x; otherwise plain-dash in the last 80ms if a cell is left. (2) If red or a rail end lies within 0.45s of travel on the current rail: snap to the rail (current or adjacent) with the longest clear run over the next 8 cells, preferring one that keeps a lock; if none, strike or plain-dash across. (3) If a lancer is charging on the current rail and cannot be killed before it fires: leave the rail. (4) If a shielder is ahead on the same rail: snap to the adjacent rail with a clear run. (5) If a lock exists with a safe landing: strike when the target is within 5 cells (attract mode waits for the nearest eighth-note for style, and takes Close Cuts when safe). A 1.2s forward simulation of the chosen action vetoes anything that ends in a hit. The same bot runs at build time over every pattern at every phase speed and in Overdrive speed to prove each pattern is solvable.

## Tech notes
Simulation: fixed 120 Hz step, render interpolation, seeded RNG, all gameplay state in plain arrays so it can be stepped headlessly for the bot and the pattern validator. Collision is discrete and cheap: three rail heights, rail segments as x intervals, entities as small AABBs or circles (hazard boxes shrunk 15%, player hurtbox 0.35 by 0.7 cells), the strike line as a segment-versus-AABB test against red objects. Rendering: WebGL2, one dynamic vertex buffer of quads (position, uv, color) using premultiplied alpha so additive glow (alpha 0) and normal blending share a single draw call; lines are stretched quads sampling a procedural soft-edge texture; glow sprites from a generated radial gradient; font atlas rasterized once at boot to a texture. Scene renders to an RGBA16F target (RGBA8 fallback), bright pass, 5-level dual-Kawase bloom at half resolution, then one composite shader: up to 4 shockwave rings, radial chromatic aberration, glitch slices, barrel distortion, scanlines, vignette, palette uniforms for phase and Overdrive. Trails are ribbon strips from a position history; afterimages are re-drawn poses; enemy bisection partitions each enemy's primitive list by side of the slash line; particles are a pooled struct-of-arrays of 4000 updated on the CPU. Audio: Web Audio with a 100ms look-ahead scheduler, oscillators and gains per voice, a prebuilt noise buffer, feedback delays, a generated impulse for reverb, sidechain by gain automation. Cheap: everything gameplay-side, lines, trails, palette swaps. Expensive and to be budgeted: full-resolution bloom, overdraw from stacked large glow sprites, too many simultaneous oscillators (cap voices, reuse), and float render targets on weak GPUs.

## Biggest risk
The auto-locking strike could collapse into mashing one button with no decisions. Mitigations are built into the rules: a strike with no lock is a plain dash that spends one of two cells and has 0.25s recovery, so mashing leaves you empty and stuck exactly when a void or a drone arrives; red mines and walls block strike lines and shielders refuse same-rail locks, so the rail must be chosen first; bait placements make the nearest target the wrong one; lancers punish staying put; contact damage punishes pressing late. The always-visible reticle and lock line keep these decisions readable rather than hidden. A secondary risk is spectacle burying hazards, handled by the strict color law, hazards drawn above all particles, and capped shake. The validator bot catches unfair pattern combinations before they ship.

## Why this wins
It inverts the runner premise with one sentence anyone can repeat: the obstacles are the road. One button delivers a huge, never-whiffing payoff within the first five seconds, and the same button stays deep for minutes because positioning, timing and target choice sit underneath it. The three systems feed each other with no spare parts: Snap aims the Strike, Strike kills forge shields and music, shields ignite Overdrive, and Overdrive turns the one thing you could not cut into more fuel. It is built for the production constraints: discrete rails and targets are fully analyzable for a bot, and a side view of glowing slashes, trails, bisected silhouettes and constellation lines is the most beautiful thing a primitive-only neon renderer can draw. Aggression equals armor equals soundtrack, so the game sounds and looks better the better you play, and a 1-second restart puts the first slash two seconds away.

## Juice
- Kill: 50ms hitstop (90ms on Freighter cores), then time at 35% for 90ms easing back during the hang.
- Kill: the enemy's primitives are split by the sign of their side of the slash line; the two halves fly apart perpendicular to the cut with spin and cool from white to amber to dark.
- Strike: the camera spring lets the runner lunge ahead on screen and catches up in 0.25s; +4% zoom punch and 1 degree of roll on each kill.
- Kill: a shockwave ring distortion expands from the cut point (post-process ring uniform) with a 120ms radial chromatic aberration burst.
- Chain: each strike's trail segment persists 1.2s, so an aerial chain leaves a drawn constellation; kill bells climb the D minor pentatonic with the chain.
- Snap: lightning ribbon between rails, 3 afterimages, the destination rail wobbles like a plucked string and brightens.
- Shield forged: a ring contracts onto the runner, the scarf grows a segment, a rising fifth stab plays, and the new music layer enters on the next beat.
- Hit: 80ms white flash on the runner, horizontal glitch-slice displacement for 0.3s, the scarf loses a segment that flutters away, the whole mix slams through a 300 Hz low-pass for 0.6s and a music layer drops out.
- Speed: rail dashes stream faster, background stars stretch into streaks above 12 cells/s, vignette tightens, zoom widens.
- Beat: rails, windows and target brackets pulse on the kick; hats flicker the billboard glyphs.
- Overdrive ignition: one beat of silence with the bloom sucked inward, then a full-screen flash, every red flips to gold, the track transposes up a whole tone and the lead enters.
- Landing after a hang: a spray of cyan sparks along the rail, a sub thump, the legs compress for 3 frames.
